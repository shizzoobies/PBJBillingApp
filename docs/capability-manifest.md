# PB&J Strategic Accounting - Capability Manifest

This document is the AI assistant's complete knowledge of what the app can and cannot do, sent to the model as system context. Any commit that adds, changes or removes a user-facing feature MUST update it in the same commit.

Audience: the assistant talks to the firm OWNER. Staff (bookkeepers) see a reduced app; owner-only abilities are marked.

## How durations are displayed (site-wide rule)

Two formats, chosen by what the screen is for:

- **REPORTING surfaces show two-decimal hours - always x.xx.** Client Recap, the payroll Hours report (summary and detail), the Dashboard and Reports summary cards, Productivity, client month totals, the workspace summary strip and invoice line detail. "20.22h", "1.00h", "0.50h" - never one decimal and never a bare integer.
- **Live time-ENTRY and APPROVAL surfaces keep hours-and-minutes** ("1h 20m", "23m", "45s"): the Time page entry rows and split allocations, the running timer, the Timesheet week/day rows, Time Approvals, the submit-timesheet prompt, the log-time modal, per-entry and per-session rows on a client, and the "time logged" line on a task or case.

The displayed hours ARE the costing input: labor cost is the two-decimal hours shown x the pay rate. The full rule is under the payroll Hours report below ("HOW LABOR COST IS CALCULATED").

## Navigation map

Sidebar pages: those grouped in the next paragraph (Engagements, Letters and Updates are owner only). A billing-month picker, notification bell, and account menu sit in the top bar on every page.

**The owner's sidebar is grouped into sections**. In order: Dashboard, **Engagements** (Proposals, Engagements, Letters), **Clients** (Clients, Contacts, Client Recap), **Billing** (Invoices, Invoice Recap, Plans), **Operations** (Time, Timesheet, Time Approvals, Checklists, Board, Delayed, Gantt), Team, **Reports** (Reports, Productivity), Updates, **Settings** (Settings, To 100%). Dashboard, Team and Updates stand alone.

**Staff keep a flat sidebar** of eight links; no route changed.

- New-version prompt: a tab left open across a deploy shows a small "A new version of the app is ready - Refresh" toast (bottom-right) within minutes, or at once when you return to it. If a fix "isn't showing", refresh first - an old tab runs the old app.
- Out-of-date tab protection: a tab open while someone else changed something cannot save its copy of the workspace over the newer data; it shows "This tab is out of date" with a Reload button; reloading is the only way forward (unsaved typing must be re-entered). Rare: tabs sync, so mostly after a tab sat idle or lost its connection. Owners only (staff never save the whole workspace).
- Most list/board pages have an instant search box filtering by name and key fields, with a live result count, matched-text highlight and a clear button. Coverage: Clients (name/contact/email/billing type), Contacts (name/title/email/phone/company emails/linked client), Plans (name/notes), Team (name/email/role), Checklists & Gantt (task title/client - composes with the assignee/client/status filters), Delayed (task title/client/waiting note), and the Board (client/task title - composes with the Report period). Reports and Productivity remain aggregate views with their own filters.
- **Search results skip inactive clients.** A typed search never returns rows of a client marked inactive - their checklists, board rows, gantt bars, delayed items, recurring templates and linked contacts stop matching. With no search typed, lists are unchanged (history stays visible); the Clients page shows inactive clients on its Inactive / All tabs. The "Add from existing" template picker on a client page never offers retired clients' templates.
- Report period (shared date range): a "Report period" control on the Time, Timesheet, Board and Checklists pages - a preset (Today, This week, This month, This quarter, This year to date) or Custom with From/To date pickers - filters those four views; your last-used range is remembered per user (in your browser; a preset re-anchors to today on load). Separate from the top-bar billing-month picker (invoicing only).

## Dashboard

- At-a-glance cards: overdue tasks, due this week, stuck cases, unbilled hours. Each links to its page.
- "Your queue": checklist items assigned to you that need action. Skipped tasks are left out of it, the task cards, Team overview counts, Watching, Cases in flight, Stuck cases and My cases.
- Team overview (owner): each member's open/overdue counts, last active, link to their tasks.
- Cases in flight: multi-step cases with current step, who holds it, and a Stuck badge when blocked. Click to open.
- **"Skipped and pushed tasks to review (N)" (owner only)** - this year's skipped recurring tasks AND those pushed to a new date, newest first: task, client, who did it, when, which of the three reasons, and the written explanation. A pushed row reads "Pushed to &lt;date&gt;", a skipped one "Skipped". Each row's "Reviewed" clears it; **reviewing keeps the record** (who reviewed it and when; never deleted). Hidden when nothing needs review; never shown to a bookkeeper or accountant. See "Skipping a recurring task" and "Pushing a recurring task to a new date" under Checklists.
- **"Invoices past due (N)" (owner only)** - every past-due invoice across ALL months, oldest first: number, client, date sent, days past due, total, link to Invoices. Same rule as the month run's **Past due** tab; one with a failed payment is left out here (it has its own tab). Hidden when nothing is past due; never shown to a bookkeeper or in preview.
- Recent activity feed (owner).
- Quick actions: New task, Invite bookkeeper, Add client, Notifications.
- "Viewing as" (owner): preview the app exactly as a specific bookkeeper sees it (exit anytime).
  - **Every page built for preview shows the previewed person's own data**, not the owner's - the Invoice Recap (only the clients that person's team is on), the notification bell and its unread badge, "Waiting on you", the item-deletion and pending-edit approval queues, a case timeline, a client's notes, and a team member's activity log. Entering or leaving preview refreshes them all, no reload.
  - **Owner-only pages refuse in preview.** Firm settings, the AI assistant, invoices, the client recap and the feature tracker answer firm-wide and a bookkeeper cannot open them, so in preview they say they cannot load, as she would see.
  - Anything not built for preview **refuses to answer rather than showing the owner's data** - including naming somebody the app cannot find: preview refuses instead of falling back to the owner. A panel that says it cannot load in preview is deliberate - report it and it will be taught about preview.
  - Entering a preview is written to the activity log, against the owner who started it. Preview stays strictly read-only: nothing can be changed from inside it.

## Time tracking (Time page)

- Live timer: pick employee -> client -> task -> describe the work -> start/stop. The most accurate way to log time.
- **Client, task and detail are REQUIRED before time can be logged.** Nothing is auto-filled: the description box starts empty and the app never invents a "standard" note.
  - **Starting a timer is still instant** - start the clock with nothing filled in and fill the fields while it runs (from the Time page, a client's Track time button, or the **top-bar timer control, on every screen**). The running clock stays in the top bar as you move around; click it to go to the Time page to stop and log. The top-bar control can also start administrative time; group time still starts from the Time page.
  - The rule bites at **Stop & log**: anything missing blocks the save with a prompt under each field that needs it ("Add a detail to log this time."). **The timer keeps running and no tracked time is lost**; answer the prompts and Stop & log works.
  - Manual entries follow the same rule (on top of the required "why manually?" reason). Administrative time has no client or task by definition, but **still needs a note**. A multi-client (group) timer block is covered by its member clients but needs a task and a detail like any entry; "Log time manually" with Split across clients needs only the detail.
  - **The client field starts on "Choose client"** - a placeholder you can't re-select once a client is picked, on the live timer and "Log time manually". **Start timer stays grayed out until a client is picked** (it says why on hover) - otherwise starting is still instant, with no task and no detail. Administrative time and "bill to multiple clients" don't show it.
  - **The form clears itself once the time is logged** - client (back to "Choose client"), task, detail, the administrative box and any group selection. A blocked or refused Stop & log clears nothing.
  - Editing an entry can't blank a detail that was filled in. Older entries with a blank description still load and stay editable (minutes, client and date included).
- **AD HOC TIME - one-off work outside what the client is scoped for.** Tick **"Ad hoc (outside scoped work)"** and the time bills on its own invoice line at that person's rate, not inside the month's hours.
  - Anyone who logs time can set it, on all three surfaces: the live timer (tickable while it runs; the answer rides on the timer to the stop and survives a refresh), "Log time manually", and the edit form on a saved entry. Flagged entries show an "Ad hoc" chip in Recent time.
  - Administrative time never offers it; marking an entry administrative clears the flag.
  - Splitting or adjusting a split carries the flag onto every slice.
  - **The owner has the final say at review** and decides per line what to do with it on the invoice.
  - Internal (non-billable) ad hoc time still doesn't bill, like any internal time.
- The TASK box on the Time page - in the live timer AND "Log time manually" - is pick-or-type, not a fixed dropdown, suggesting:
  - that client's open tasks (picking one attaches the time to the real checklist),
  - that client's UPCOMING recurring tasks, shown as "Name (upcoming)" - choosing one generates the instance now and attaches to it,
  - every STANDARD task in the workspace (the client-agnostic blueprints on the Checklists page's "Standard" tab), and
  - anything you TYPE. A name not in the list is saved as the entry's free-text task name, shown in the Task column on the Time page lists, in daily/weekly approvals, and in the Raw report export.
  Suggestions are de-duplicated by name, the client's real task winning a tie; an empty box means no task.
- Track time for a single client, or SPLIT it across multiple clients - available to EVERYONE who logs time (not just owners); the server enforces that every client picked is one that person is allowed to bill.
  - Log time manually -> pick "A group", choose the clients and how to divide the block (evenly, a custom minutes-per-client amount, or the full duration to each). Saving creates one billable entry per client in a single action (a live preview shows each share). An optional Task box (a standard task or your own words) names every entry.
  - **A tracked group block can be EDITED before it is split** - start/stop times, duration, date, description or billable - and stays a group block across all its clients. No need to pick a single client first: the client box offers "Keep as group time (N clients) - split it below"; picking an actual client is optional and collapses the block to that client.
  - Live timer -> pick "A group" and the clients, track the block, then "Split across clients" on the saved entry (in Recent time), the same ways. (Splitting a running timer happens after stop.) A group timer has a Task box (a standard task or your own words) and **Stop & log requires it**, like a single entry; every split entry copies it and shows it in the Task column (Time lists, approvals, Raw/detail reports); Hours by task groups typed tasks by name, any case. Older group blocks keep no task until you pick each entry's task in its edit form.
  - ANY client time entry can be split after the fact, whether or not it started as a "group": every entry in Sent back / Recent time has "Split across clients" (also in its edit form, next to Save) - a checkbox list of the clients that person may bill, the current client ticked. Tick the others, choose how to divide it, confirm.
    - Splitting to a SINGLE client is refused on purpose: that's moving the entry, so use the Client dropdown in the edit form.
    - Administrative time can't be split - it has no client. Give it one first.
    - The replacement entries go back into the daily approval queue as pending, even if the original was already approved (same rule as any other edit to an approved entry). Billable / internal is carried over.
  - A SPLIT CAN BE ADJUSTED AFTERWARD. Any entry from a split shows "Adjust split" (in Recent time / Sent back, and its edit form) instead of "Split across clients". It reopens the split as saved: clients ticked, each one's exact minutes filled in, the saved mode preselected, the current total shown as "was". Change amounts, add or remove clients, switch modes, save. Switching a reopened split to **By percentage** fills the boxes with what is billed today (36m and 24m of an hour show as 60% and 40%).
    - THE TOTAL MAY CHANGE: unlike creating a split, an adjustment need not add up to the original block - it is an explicit correction of what gets billed. The clock-in / clock-out times stay as the record of the time worked.
    - Adjusting down to ONE client is allowed (pulling a client back out of a split); creating a one-client "split" is still refused.
    - The whole group is replaced in one step and keeps its identity (still one split), and every adjusted entry goes back through approval. Adjustments are written to the activity log (who adjusted it, how many clients, old total -> new total).
  - Editing ONE entry of a split (a typo in the notes, the date, the client) never disturbs the split: it keeps its own share and stays in the group. Resume / Add time on one still adds exactly the time added. To change how the time is DIVIDED, use "Adjust split".
  - Splitting is ATOMIC: the per-client entries are created and the source entry removed in ONE step.
  - Each split entry KEEPS the block's clock-in / clock-out (manual group entries too) and its task - a checklist task stays on its own client's entry, the others show its name - while billing only its own share of the minutes. (Entries split from 2026-09-23 on; earlier split rows keep what they had.) Adjusting a split keeps each client's own task.
  - Every split is written to the activity log (who split what, how many clients, how many hours).
  - HOW THE SPLIT IS DIVIDED - the modal leads with the two easy answers:
    - **Evenly** - the same share of the block to every client.
    - **By percentage** - type each client's share ("60% / 40%"). The boxes open on an even percentage each, each percentage shows in TIME as you type ("60% - 36m"), and a running line says whether it adds up ("Adds up to 95% - 5% left"); it can't be saved until the percentages total exactly 100.
    - **Exact minutes** - in the compact row underneath: type each client's minutes. Needed for seconds-precision corrections; a saved split reopens on it.
    - **Full duration to each** - deliberately different: every client is billed the whole block (a meeting that serves several clients).
  - Percentages are a friendlier way to SAY a custom split: converted to exact seconds and saved as a normal custom split, so nothing downstream (approval, payroll, invoicing, reports) sees a new kind of entry.
  - A CUSTOM / percentage split must add up EXACTLY to the tracked block - to the second, not the minute. Percentages totaling 100 always convert to seconds that add up: the leftover seconds of an awkward block (33.33% of 45m 20s) are handed out one at a time. In exact-minutes mode the modal shows what is still unassigned (or over) with a one-click button giving the remainder to the last client; it can't be saved until it balances. Even splits divide to the exact second and always add up.
- Administrative work toggle: internal/company time with no client or task.
- Log time manually: same fields plus date and duration, for after-the-fact entries. Manual entries are flagged for owner approval ("manual" badge) and notify the owner.
- "Get ahead" tasks: picking an "(upcoming)" task in the task box (above); staff can do this for their assigned clients.
- Recent time list: edit or delete your recent entries, scoped by the shared Report period (defaults to this month; the live timer and log form aren't affected). It shows EVERY entry in range, uncapped.
- Both Time-page lists - "Sent back" and "Recent time" - collapse from a chevron in their heading and each scrolls in its own box.
- Editing an entry changes EVERY field, not just the time: client, task, date, description, billable, the administrative toggle, the work sessions AND the hours/minutes duration, and - for owners - which team member it belongs to. A different client clears a task from the old client (the task list follows the client); switching to administrative drops its client/task and makes it non-billable.
- THE TIME ITSELF IS EDITABLE TWO WAYS, on every entry, timer captures included: the WORK SESSIONS (clock in / clock out) - when the work happened - and an HOURS + MINUTES duration - what gets BILLED. They normally agree, so changing a clock time updates the duration. But TYPING A DURATION WINS: the entry bills exactly what was typed, and the clock times stay untouched. A hint under the field shows what it will bill and whether from the sessions or from you.
- EDIT THE TIME FIRST, THEN SPLIT IT. Adjusting an entry from 60 to 45 minutes and then splitting it divides 45 - a split always divides the entry's current billed time, each slice keeping the original clock-in / clock-out. Retyping one slice's duration likewise bills that amount without restoring the whole block or disturbing its siblings.
- Typing a duration on a TIMER entry does not make it a manual entry - it is an edit, through the normal approval queue; no manual reason is required.
- Saving an edit RESUBMITS the entry for approval: a rejected entry goes back to pending, and so does an approved one (a changed entry must be re-approved rather than keep its old sign-off). A save that changes nothing leaves the approval state alone. Bookkeepers still can't edit a locked month; owners are exempt, and non-owners can only move an entry onto a client they're assigned to.
- Billable vs non-billable is determined by the client's billing setup.
- Weekly-submission gate: staff must SUBMIT (or resubmit) a prior week that has logged time before logging NEW time - dated in the CURRENT week or a future one. A prior week blocks when it's un-submitted OR was sent back for changes (rejected), with a message naming the week to submit/fix. A week already submitted (pending owner approval) or approved does NOT block, so an awaiting-approval week never locks them out.
- Catching up on a PAST week is always allowed: adding a forgotten entry to, or fixing one in, any week that has already ended never hits this gate, even if an older week is un-submitted or was sent back. Only a LOCKED timesheet month, which an owner must unlock, stops a past-week change.
- **Guided "Submit timesheet" flow**: the submit button on the Time and Timesheet pages asks about one week at a time. Its prompt works PAST WEEKS FIRST: if any earlier week still needs submitting, it auto-selects the OLDEST, names it ("Submitting week of Sun Jul 26 - Sat Aug 1") with its hours, and says how many more are queued ("2 more past weeks still need submitting after this one"). Confirming sends that one week; the prompt then advances to the next oldest, and can be closed at any point. A SENT BACK week appears in the same queue as a resubmit and says so.
- When nothing prior is outstanding, the prompt says "All past weeks are submitted - nothing prior to submit." and then asks the completion question: "Are you finished logging time for the current week (Sun Aug 9 - Sat Aug 15)? Submitting sends it to the owner for approval." - Yes submits it, "Not yet" closes. **That explicit yes is the only way the current week gets submitted from the app.** The prompt's list of outstanding past weeks uses the SAME rule as the weekly-submission gate above (logged time, un-submitted or sent back, sealed months excluded).
- If nothing prior is outstanding AND the current week can't be submitted (already pending or approved, or its month is locked), the prompt says so and offers only "Done". The server still accepts a submission for any week, so an owner can still ask someone to submit early.
- **The "Submit timesheet" button grays out once there is nothing to send** On both pages it is disabled, with a tooltip saying why, whenever the click has nothing to submit: "Submitted - awaiting review." for a pending week, "Approved - this week is closed." for an approved one, a locked-month message when the month is sealed, and "Nothing left to submit - every week you owe is in." otherwise. The reason quoted is the week ON SCREEN, so paging to a week still owed lights the button back up.
- Two cases keep the button live. A SENT BACK week stays clickable - the resubmit path. And a week pending or approved while an OLDER week is still owed keeps it enabled (that older week is what the weekly gate blocks new time on); the tooltip then names the week the click would send ("This week is submitted and awaiting review. Submitting sends the week of Sun Aug 2 - Sat Aug 8 instead."). That naming applies whenever the click would send a week OTHER than the one on screen, including when the viewed week is itself owed but an older one is queued ahead of it ("Submitting sends the week of Sun Aug 2 - Sat Aug 8 first.").
- Signed out, the button reads "Sign in to submit a timesheet." rather than claiming the person is caught up.
- Inside the prompt, the confirm button disables itself and reads "Submitting…" while the request is open.
- None of this is UI-only: a weekly submission is one row per person per week, so re-submitting the same week can never create a second one, and an already-approved week is returned untouched rather than knocked back to pending.

## Timesheet page

- Day-by-day view of what each person worked on, scoped by the shared Report period, with a total. Owner can switch between team members; staff see their own.
- Single-week mode (Report period = This week, or a one-week range): navigate weeks with ◀ ▶ arrows or "This week", and the approval / lock status for that week shows alongside. The "Submit timesheet" button opens the guided flow (above), which starts with the oldest past week still owed, not the week viewed.
- Multi-week range, or a one-day Today range: the day list + total are read-only (no Submit/lock); pick a single week to submit or lock.
- **Every duration on the page is BILLED time, not clock time**. Each row still shows the clock-in -> clock-out it came from, but the minutes beside it are that entry's own billed minutes. For a SPLIT block, each slice keeps the whole original block's clock-in/out as its audit trail, so a 25-minute block split across 20 clients shows ~1m 15s per client and a 25-minute day - not 25 minutes twenty times. For a hand-corrected entry, the page reports the corrected duration with the untouched clock times beside it. Day, range and week totals are sums of those billed minutes.

## Time Approvals (owner only)

- **TABBED sections.** The page's three areas - **Weekly submissions**, **Approval queue** and **Timesheet locks** - are tabs, one click each. Each tab label shows its pending count: weeks awaiting review, entries awaiting approval, and people not yet locked for the month shown in the locks table (0 for a month that hasn't ended, since those can't be locked). The page OPENS on the first tab with pending work, or on Weekly submissions when everything is clear. The open tab is in the URL (`?section=weekly|queue|locks`) so it can be linked and survives a refresh, and an old `#weekly-submissions` / `#approval-queue` / `#timesheet-locks` link opens the matching tab.
- **What needs per-entry approval**: a pure TIMER capture is auto-approved the moment it's saved - never in the daily approval queue; the weekly submission / month lock reviews it as a whole. Individual daily approval is reserved for time a person TYPED: manual entries (which always carry a reason), the per-client allocations from splitting a group time block, and any entry EDITED afterwards (an edited approved entry re-queues as pending - see the Time page).
- **AD HOC - the owner's backstop.** Every entry in the approval queue carries an **"Ad hoc (outside scoped work)"** tick: employees set it when logging; review adds a missed one or takes a wrong one off. It saves when ticked (no approve needed). **Only owners can change it on somebody else's time** (enforced on the server, not just hidden). Administrative entries have no tick. **An owner ticking it does NOT un-approve an already-approved entry** - the flag changes how the work bills, not the record, and the person ticking it is the approver. (Every other edit still re-queues, as does this one if a bookkeeper changes it on their own approved time, or an owner changes anything else with it.)
- Weekly submissions: staff submit a week; approving seals every pending entry in it. Rejecting unlocks the week so the bookkeeper can edit and resubmit.
- Reopen an approved week (undo an approval): a "Recently approved" list shows the latest approved weeks, each with "Reopen", which un-approves the week - the submission goes back to pending (re-entering the review queue) and its sealed entries become pending and editable again. (If the month is ALSO locked, unlock it in the Month-end section - the two are independent; unlocking a month is what lets staff edit that month's time.)
- Weekly review modal: expand any individual entry; per-entry actions - "Approve this entry" or "Send back with note" (the note is required and the bookkeeper sees it). The owner does not edit staff time directly.
- Sending an entry back NOTIFIES the bookkeeper: an in-app bell + email naming the client, the date, the hours and the owner's reason, linking to the Time page to edit and resubmit.
- A team member sees ALL of their own time - client work for their assigned clients, administrative time, AND unsplit GROUP holding entries. A group block has no single client (its members sit in the group list until it's split for billing). Only the member COUNT is shown, never the member client names.
- "SENT BACK" section: a panel at the top of the Time page listing every one of YOUR entries an owner returned, to find and fix in one place. Unlike Recent time it is NOT scoped by the report period and NOT capped. Oldest first, each with the owner's reason and the normal editor; "Edit & resubmit" sends it straight back for approval without resubmitting the week. Hidden entirely when nothing is sent back.
- "N sent back" badge: individual entries can be returned while the WEEK's submission stays "pending", so the Time page week bar and the Timesheet week controls both show a red "N sent back" count for that week whenever any of its entries are rejected. Editing a sent-back entry resubmits it (back to pending) automatically.
- Every approval surface (the week-review list and the individual approval queue) shows CLOCK IN -> CLOCK OUT for each entry, with per-session rows and each session's length when a day was split. Timer entries and older ones predating the sessions model show their start/stop stamps.
- Approval queue: filter Pending / Rejected / All individual submitted entries.
- Timesheet locks: lock a month per employee - pending entries are auto-approved and the employee can no longer change that month. "Lock all" locks everyone at once. Locking is the month-end sign-off. Only a month that has ALREADY ENDED can be locked - locking the current or a future month would block everyone from tracking time in it (the per-row Lock and "Lock all" are hidden for such months; the server rejects it too). Unlocking any month always works.

## Checklists (tasks)

- **Overdue work is PINNED to the top of the page, above everything.** When you have anything past due, a slim red "Overdue" bar with a count is the first thing on the Checklists page - collapsed by default (on every visit). Click it to expand one row per late task: the business, the task, when it was due, how many days late, and whose task it is, longest-overdue first.
  - **Nothing can bury it.** It sits above the tabs, so it shows on every tab, and ignores the assignee/client/status filters, the search box, the group-by choice and the report period - those narrow the list below, never the panel (so its count can differ from the list's "Overdue" section).
  - **Clicking a row takes you to that task's card** on the In progress tab, clearing the filters and search box and showing the task even outside the report period. **It does not change your report period** (shared with the Timesheet, where a changed range would break the weekly submit); only the task you jumped to is let through.
  - Late tasks appear **twice** on purpose: in the panel (the nudge) and in the list (where you work).
  - **When nothing is overdue the panel is absent** - no "all caught up" banner.
  - Each person sees their own scope: staff their own overdue tasks, the owner the whole firm's.
- **The page is split into four TABS: "In progress", "Repeating", "Standard" and "Completed"**, each with a count.
  - **In progress** - the live checklists, with the "Group by: Due date / Client" choice and collapsible Overdue / Due this week / Due this month / Later / Completed sections. By Client, each client lists its open checklists (count = open) and folds completed ones into a collapsed "Completed" sub-group at the bottom; a client with only completed work starts collapsed, its count reading "N done".
  - **Repeating** - the recurring task setups (owner edits them here; staff see the recurring checklists for their assigned clients).
  - **Standard** - the firm's reusable blueprint templates.
  - **Completed** - the history of finished tasks.
  - On "Repeating", tasks are grouped under their business, alphabetically, and **each business starts COLLAPSED** - a scannable list of business names with a count each; click one to open its tasks (a search, or a link to a repeating task, opens the matching businesses).
  - **In progress / Repeating / Standard each have a search box** (Completed has filters instead). "Repeating" and "In progress" match the BUSINESS or task name; "Standard" matches template names. Each shows "N of M" while you type; Escape clears it.
  - Each tab's count is what you'd see: "In progress" applies the report period and the assignee/client/status filters; if it looks low, the period is usually why (a narrow range hides everything outside it).
  - **Status filter: All / Active / Overdue / Waiting / Completed.** Waiting shows checklists with at least one open step that is waiting, in the Delayed page's sense: the waiting flag is on, or a wait on it has not been approved yet (a ticked step does not count). It is read from the steps each time, so clearing or approving the wait takes it out. Overdue and Active are unchanged: a checklist both waiting and overdue shows under both. Its card has a small "N waiting" badge (hover: "N steps or sub-steps waiting"); a step ticked with an open sub-step still counts as open.
  - The "+ New" button stays available from all four tabs. Links to a specific task or repeating setup open the right tab. The recycle bin sits below the tabs and is always available (owner only).
- **Completed tasks tab - the record of finished work.** A finished task stays where it was, filtered out of the active lists. This tab shows them newest first: WHAT was completed, for WHICH client, by WHOM, and WHEN.
  - **Read-only** (no checkboxes, editing or re-opening here); re-open a task on the In progress tab, under the usual permission rules.
  - **Who sees what:** an employee sees their own completed tasks; an **Accountant** also sees those of the people staffed on the clients they're assigned to; the owner sees everything. (The same rule as the open-tasks count. There is no supervisor field in the data, so "their bookkeepers" means "the people on the same clients" - everywhere in the app.)
  - **Filters:** client, person, and a completed-from / completed-to date range.
  - **"Completed by" is the person responsible for the task.** Completing a step requires being its responsible person, so that is who finished it (which account clicked is not recorded).
  - **Dates before this feature show "-", not a guess.** Completion times were not recorded before this tab and cannot be recovered (a note under the table says why); everything completed since carries a real timestamp. A date range hides the dashed rows (an unknown date can't be said to fall inside a window), and the tab says so.
- **THE PERIOD A TASK'S WORK COVERS, shown beside its title on the checklist card.** A recurring task can carry a small label naming the period it is FOR - "July 13 - August 13, 2026" on a task due at the end of August.
  - **Off unless turned on for that repeating task** - like skipping, a setting on the repeating setup ("Show the period it covers"), chosen at creation and changeable later on the Repeating tasks list. A task without one shows nothing (no space, no dash).
  - **You pick the dates**: beside the switch, the FIRST period the task covers - a From and a To, e.g. July 13 to August 13. Nothing else to fill in.
  - **It advances on its own, by however often the task repeats**: the next reads "August 13 - September 13, 2026", then "September 13 - October 13, 2026", nothing to reset. A quarterly task steps a quarter, a yearly one a year. It reads like covered dates on a reimbursed expense.
  - **Changing the dates re-anchors from the next occurrence on.** Occurrences already created keep the label they were born with.
  - **IT IS ONLY A LABEL.** It changes nothing: not the due date, not which month work is billed in, not any report, total, filter or sort.
  - **How the dates move:** the window you type lands on the occurrence in front of you and moves forward by the recipe's own schedule - a some-months-of-the-year recipe steps by the months it runs, a full calendar month stays a full month, and a longer window keeps its length. Saving a recipe's dates updates its open tasks right away; finished tasks keep the period they had.
- **THE APP'S DAY IS THE FIRM'S DAY, US EASTERN.** The server runs on UTC, already tomorrow from 8 pm Eastern, so every place the app decides the day or month uses the firm's day and rolls over at midnight Eastern: the weekly time-logging check, past-due invoices and their notices, the default period on reports and recaps, the assistant's own "today", the weekly digest, the month lock, the month a new client or a new rate starts in, repeating tasks, and invoice issue and due dates. An invoice's due date counts from the firm's day it was generated or sent. The Invoice Date printed on an invoice follows the firm's day for invoices sent from 2 October 2026 on; earlier copies keep the date they were printed with.
- **A REPEATING TASK STARTS THE DAY IT IS SET UP. Past occurrences are not created.** Setting one up (by hand, or with "Set up plan checklists" on a client) produces its first task on or after that day. A some-months recipe set up in September opens with September, not older months to delete; a weekly or monthly one starts at its first cycle from today, not an old blueprint date.
  - **Tasks that already exist are never touched** - only ones that would have been created from now on.
  - **"The day" is the firm's day, US Eastern** (see "THE APP'S DAY IS THE FIRM'S DAY"). The next month's task appears at midnight Eastern, not earlier in the evening. An evening set-up counts as that day, so a recipe set up on the last evening of a month gets that month's task if it is due that day or later.
  - **One occurrence that is already due still appears.** A monthly task set up today and dated the 1st of this month gives you this month's task - a date you chose, not a back-fill. What is skipped is a run of them: a recipe whose date came from a blueprint months back.
  - **Choosing a first due date yourself still wins** - used exactly as typed, past or not.
- **Skipping a recurring task (a "quiet skip").** When someone won't complete a recurring task this cycle but will catch it on the next occurrence, they can step past this one instead of letting it sit flagged as overdue.
  - **Skipping is OFF unless it was turned on for that repeating task** - a setting on the repeating setup itself ("Allow skipping an occurrence…"), chosen at creation and changeable later on the Repeating tab; setups are owner-managed, so the owner decides. **With it off there is no skip button** (not even grayed out).
  - **One-off tasks are never skippable** - there is no next occurrence to catch them on.
  - **What the person doing it sees:** a "Skip this cycle" button on the task card and one small form - a required dropdown for **who couldn't complete it (me / a colleague / the client)** and a required written explanation. No approval to wait for, no block: the task leaves their active list for this cycle.
  - **The next occurrence still generates as normal**, on its own due date, open and unskipped; the schedule is unchanged.
  - **A skipped task does not read as overdue anywhere** - the overdue rules are unchanged; a skipped occurrence isn't in the lists they read.
  - **Who gets told:** the owner every time, and an **Accountant** when a **Bookkeeper** skips a task on a client that accountant is assigned to. Both are ordinary in-app notifications plus the usual email, and both can be turned off per person under Settings -> Notifications ("Skipped recurring tasks").
  - **The owner reviews them on her Dashboard**; reviewing keeps the record permanently.
  - **A skip is not a deletion and not a completion.** The task is still there, attached to its time entries and history; it is out of the way for this cycle.
- **Pushing a recurring task to a new date.** When someone still intends to do a recurring task but not by its due date, they can push it - the task stays open and moves to a new due date instead of being stepped past.
  - **"Push to a new date" is on every task you can edit** - recurring or one-off, whatever the skip setting: beside "Skip this cycle" where skipping is on, alone elsewhere. **Skip is unchanged**: still only where an owner turned it on for that repeating setup, never on a one-off.
  - **The form is the skip form plus a date:** a required dropdown for **who could not complete it (me / a colleague / the client)**, a required written explanation, and a **new due date pre-filled with the next cycle** of that task's own schedule. The date is editable to anything after the current due date (a push only ever moves a task forward; the picker starts the next day), and may not land more than **two years** out: further is almost always a mistyped year, and a genuinely distant task can be pushed twice.
  - **It is not a completion and not a skip.** Nothing is checked off, nothing is unblocked, no next step is started. The open work lives on at its new date.
  - **A push carries only the open work.** Done steps stay on the original as a finished record (complete, in the Completed group, card says "Pushed · open steps moved to <date>"); a new copy on the new date gets only the open steps, with their sub-steps, waits, assignees and order. The Push window says which: "All N steps move to <date>. Nothing is completed." when nothing is done, or "N done steps stay here as a completed record; M open steps move to <date>." With everything done Push is not offered (the server answers "Everything here is done. Nothing to push." if asked). A step with some sub-steps ticked is open and moves whole. The new copy still counts as that month's checklist (next month's is not created early or twice); deletion requests, pending edits, client-page notes and "waiting for" links naming a moved step follow it. The finished record cannot be pushed again (push the live copy).
  - **It keeps its place in the cycle, so the next occurrence still generates.** The task remembers its original due date, and that is what the schedule counts: a task pushed from September into October does not make September generate again, and October's own occurrence is still created on time as a separate task - even on the same day.
  - **The card says so:** a pushed task reads "Pushed · was &lt;date&gt;" beside its due date.
  - **Who gets told:** exactly the same people as a skip, same in-app notice plus email, same per-person switch under Settings -> Notifications ("Skipped and pushed recurring tasks").
  - **The owner reviews pushes on her Dashboard,** in the same list as skips ("Skipped and pushed tasks to review").
  - **Still open, not built:** whether a push should apply to a single step rather than the whole task, and whether pushing a step in a multi-step case should trigger the next step. Today all open steps move together and nothing is triggered.
- **When a team member creates a task, the owner is notified.** The notice names the task and the person.
- **When a step is completed is now recorded.** Ticking a step stamps the completion time; un-ticking it (or adding a sub-step to a finished item, which re-opens it) clears the stamp. The stamp survives ordinary saves - a background workspace save can neither erase a completion date nor invent one.
- A checklist = a task for a client: title, client, assignee, due date, frequency (one-off, weekly, monthly, quarterly, annual), steps.
- Steps support sub-steps and sub-sub-steps, drag-to-reorder, per-step due date and per-step assignee ("Same as checklist" by default), and checkboxes.
- **Reordering, completed steps and Hide completed.** Steps drag by the grip on the left, sub-steps too (within their step); every open step and sub-step also has Move up / Move down arrows, and the order saves. Finished steps sit below the open ones: ticking moves a step down, unticking returns it to its saved place, and reordering only moves open steps. "Hide completed (N)" at the top of a checklist's steps folds finished steps (and finished sub-steps of an open step) away, showing "N completed steps hidden - Show"; remembered per checklist, per browser, not shared, and shown only when something is finished. A finished step with a wait still awaiting approval, on it or beneath it, is never hidden. Only people who can edit the checklist can reorder; anyone who can see it can hide completed steps.

An owner's choice acts at once; a staff one sends the owner a deletion request (nothing changes until approved). **This + all future** removes the step here, ONE matching step (same name ignoring capitals and spaces, same stage) from the recurring checklist, and the same-named step from later existing checklists ONLY where untouched (not done, no sub-step done, no wait of any kind; an approved wait counts as work); the notice names each month kept and why ("Kept on November 2026 (an open wait) and December 2026 (work had started)."). Earlier, skipped and recycled checklists and other stages never change; of several same-named steps only the same-position one goes. The last step of the recurring checklist's first stage cannot go this way ("This is the last step of the recurring checklist. Delete or pause the recurring checklist instead."). A step not on the recurring checklist: the notice says only this one changed. Approving a staff "This + all future" request acts like the owner's; a refusal leaves it pending; if it changed since, approval is refused ("This request changed - reload to see it.") and only the requester can change it. A one-off checklist asks "Delete this step?". Choices lock while a delete runs; a refusal shows the server's sentence; requests or pending edits aimed at a removed step go with it.
- Group the page by due date or by client; filter by assignee, client, status (see the Status filter under the tabs).
- Waiting on (the hourglass ⏳): flag a step as waiting, write who/what on (free text), optionally pick the SPECIFIC other task it waits for - when it completes, the blocked step's assignee is notified, bell + email ("Ready to continue"). The picker offers **only this client's other tasks you can open**: no skipped occurrences, recycle-bin tasks or other people's tasks you aren't on; an internal task with no client sees only other no-client tasks. A link **already saved** stays listed and selected even if another client's (name in brackets), skipped, recycled or not yours. The server refuses, with a sentence, a link to a missing task, another client's task or itself; an ALREADY-saved link can always be re-sent. The picked task saves WITH the wait, fixed afterwards. Waiting items also show on the Delayed page.
- Resolving a waiting step - **Done vs Clear** in the waiting editor, for a step flagged waiting the free-text way with **no saved wait live on it** (once a wait is saved both disappear; see the lock below). **Done** retires the flag and keeps the note on that checklist as a "Was waiting on: …" record (that instance only - future recurring instances start fresh). Done does **NOT** check the step off; that stays with the normal checkboxes. **Clear** un-flags and erases the note. Resolved steps stop counting on the Delayed page and the Board's waiting chips. A server refusal shows its reason in red inside the editor.
- **A waiting step cannot be checked off, except by the owner.** Waiting is as in the Status filter. Staff see its checkbox grayed out, "Clear the wait first" (Checklists page, Board, client page, Dashboard queue). Not by ticking a parent ("A sub-step is waiting - clear it first"), its last open sub-step ("The step above is waiting - clear it first") or deleting it (grayed out: "Removing this would finish a step that is waiting. Clear its wait first."); an owner approving such a deletion request is refused with that sentence, the request kept (Approve grayed out when the app already knows). The server refuses these in the same words; the page refreshes. The owner (not while previewing someone) can tick a waiting step, sub-step or last open sub-step directly ("Checking this off clears the wait"): every wait it finishes is closed, verified by her. Un-ticking is never blocked and never touches a wait; clearing a wait never ticks the step. A step at any level with an open wait on or beneath it cannot be deleted by anyone, owner included, here or across a series ("This + all future"): Delete is grayed out with "This step has an open wait on it. Close the wait first (mark it done and approve it), then delete the step.", no request is filed, and an approval is refused with the request kept; an approved wait does not block.
- Waiting on a PERSON (two-way): flag a step as waiting on a specific team member. They are notified at once (bell + email) and see a "Waiting on you" card on their Dashboard with "Mark done" - which tells BOTH the step's assignee and whoever flagged it they can continue. The bell and the card's link open THEIR Delayed page ("Waiting on me", that wait marked, or a note if handled), even when the task isn't theirs; the email opens the app. A step can wait on several people independently; each is cleared (and notified) alone. You can wait on a colleague or the task's client - **never on yourself**: your name isn't in the picker, and the server refuses it ("A wait names who you are waiting ON - pick the client or a colleague, not yourself").
- **The hand-off has TWO steps, and the record is kept.** A wait moves through three states and is never destroyed:
  1. **Waiting** - amber. On the Delayed page of the person waited on, of whoever asked, and of the step's assignee (the one held up).
  2. **Done** - the person being waited on presses **Mark done**. It leaves THEIR Delayed page, notifies whoever asked, and shows green with "done by <name>" and a date, tagged "awaiting your OK".
  3. **Approved** - whoever asked presses **Approve**. The wait closes out and leaves their Delayed page too - and stays on the checklist step as a **completed sub-item**: a ticked box with the label struck through, like a task you check off, with the full record underneath ("asked by <name> <date> · done by <name> <date> · confirmed by <name> <date>"). It stays permanently: after the step is checked off or the editor closes, editable or not. The tick belongs to the WAIT, not the step: confirming a wait never checks the step itself off, and the box can't be un-ticked.
  You cannot approve work nobody has reported finished (the app says so - the wait keeps until they press Done), and the person who did the work cannot approve their own. Owners can do either step on anyone's behalf.
- **A saved wait can never be removed - by anyone.** The old **Cancel** (the x) is gone: a wait is the shared record of who asked, who did it and who confirmed. The old `cancel` request is refused server-side ("A saved wait is the record of who asked and who did it, so it stays on the task. Mark it done and approve it instead."). The way out is forward: Mark done -> Approve, or Send back; the dated record stays on the task throughout.
- **Send back - "not approved, do it again."** At step 2 the asker gets **Send back** beside Approve: a required note, and the wait goes straight back - amber, in the other person's "Waiting on me" list with a Done, notifying them like the original request. Nothing is overwritten: the original note and every send-back note are kept in order with who reported it done and when. The step is never ticked off by this.
- **Question - "what exactly do you need?"** The person being waited on gets **Question** beside Done (Delayed page AND the wait's chip on the step), opening a message box. Sending it does **NOT** complete the wait; it stays on their list. Every question is kept on the wait (attributed, timestamped, beside the send-back history); whoever asked is notified with it, as are the step's assignee and the person waited on; an owner can send one on their behalf. The LATEST question shows on both. A wait on the CLIENT has no Question: a client has no login to read one.
- **Creating a wait is Save, and it is final.** The first click on "Waiting on…" opens an editor and commits **nothing**: pick the person (or the client), type the message that goes WITH the wait, optionally choose the task it waits for - all held until **Save** writes them in one go. **Clear** (only before Save) discards the whole draft (opened by accident): nothing is created or notified, and nothing the step already had is touched.
- **Save locks everything.** Once a wait is saved, the person or client it names, its message and the task it waits for are fixed ("all info is locked and cannot be changed"). A step with a live wait shows no note box, task picker, Clear or Done; the saved task link still READS ("Waiting for: <task>"); only the wait's own controls remain: Mark done, Question, Approve, Send back. The server refuses the same (changing the waiting note or task link, or un-flagging, while a wait is live: "...are fixed. Mark it done and approve it instead."). A SECOND wait on the step still works but is no way in: no task picker, and a create that would change the locked link is refused. The step's name, due date and assignee stay as they are. The lock lifts once every wait on the step is approved.
- **Save waits for the server.** Save stays disabled until the wait is created; a refusal keeps everything typed on screen beside the reason.
- **A wait cannot be lost to an unrelated save.** Waits are written only by the waiting-on buttons; every other save (the big background one too) leaves them as the server has them, whatever the saving tab loaded.
- **A checked-off step doesn't hide a live wait** ("This step is checked off, but a wait on it is still open"), so Approve / Send back stay reachable from the step (for a step ticked before the waiting rule).
- **Waiting on the CLIENT.** The same picker offers the task's own client alongside the team. A client has no login, so there is nobody to hand back to or notify: a single press ("Heard back") by whoever flagged it or the step's assignee closes it, recording who and when.
- Recurring templates: build a template once (with steps/sub-steps); the app creates an instance each period. Frequencies: daily, weekly, biweekly, monthly, quarterly, annually, or specific months. Each template recurs on its OWN cadence (a monthly one never blocks a weekly one). Owner manages templates; "get ahead" lets staff generate the next instance early (this CREATES it). An instance due today opens with its steps open; the day used is the person's own calendar day. A day a shorter month lacks becomes its last day, never the next month (quarterly: January 31, April 30, July 31). The step sees only the date: once a recipe lands on a month's last day (a 30th in a 30-day month, a 28th or 29th in February) it runs on month ends from then on; the month itself never changes.
- Upcoming (read-only preview): the Board and Gantt can show FUTURE recurring instances not yet generated - projected from each template's recurrence rule, faded with an "Upcoming" badge. They are NOT real tasks, can't be edited/checked, and nothing is created (unlike "get ahead"). "Show upcoming" turns them on/off; the horizon is the selected Report period.
- Cases (multi-stage workflows): a template can define stages ("steps", e.g. Data entry -> Review -> Filing), each with its own assignee and due date. Step 1 appears "Show before Next due (days)" before the recipe's Next due date, due then or on its own day of the month; finishing a step creates the next (due on its own day of the month, else the previous step's day) and notifies its assignee. Stuck cases (also a finished step due in the last 90 days whose next one never opened) are flagged on the Dashboard. A tick always saves even if the next stage cannot be created (the page says so; untick and tick to retry; an unknown error is worded plainly, details in the server log); Activity says "could not advance case", or "did not advance case" if the template has no next stage, a later step is open or another case has that step on that date. **Open next step now** (owner or assignee) starts the next stage when every open step is waiting (that step stays open; Activity: "opened the next step early") or when a finished step's next one never opened. Onboarding cases are refused ("Onboarding steps move on only when they are finished").

## Board - Active Checklists (sidebar: "Board")

- Active checklists grouped **by service type** ("service categories", e.g. Monthly Bookkeeping, Sales Tax, Payroll) in a **wrap grid** (as many ~320px columns per row as fit; one on a narrow screen), scrolling vertically. Each column lists the **clients with open work** of that type.
- **Folds start closed:** a column shows only its name and client count ("Clear Feed (7)", after filters and search); click it to list its clients as folded rows (name, chips, count), then a row to open it. "Expand all" / "Collapse all" by Hide empty columns. Not remembered (a refresh closes all); none changes the groups, strip, filters or search.
- **"Current step only" is ON by default** (remembered per user and device): rows start folded in both views; an opened row shows each checklist's current step in bold - "Step N of M - <stage>" on a staged case, else the checklist's name - who it is for, "N of M tasks done", and "Then: Step N+1 - <next stage>" when a later stage exists. Tasks are NOT listed (the step is the unit, its items its tasks); "Open checklist" opens the full card; search also matches the step. Untick for the full cards with checkboxes (each view keeps its own row folds while the page is open). Completing a client's checklist **removes that client from the column**.
- **Report period** (the shared date range at the top) is a horizon: a checklist shows when due on or before the period's END, so overdue work stays visible and a larger range widens it (today -> week -> month -> quarter -> year-to-date -> custom).
- **Due vs waiting at a glance:** each checklist has a status chip - gray "Due <date>", red "Overdue - was due <date>", or amber "Waiting - <reason>" when any open step is waiting (per the Waiting filter: note or who it waits on; hover for all reasons). Client rows roll up "N waiting" / "N overdue" chips.
- **Filters:** beside the client filter, a **team-member filter** (checklists assigned to the chosen members); both multi-select, combined with search and the Report period. **"Show upcoming" defaults OFF** (real work only); tick it for faded projected items.
- **Scoping - whose work you see:** everyone has the board, showing **only the checklists you're active on** (assignee, or a viewer the owner named), on clients you're assigned to; the OWNER sees it all.
- **"Show my bookkeepers'" (accountants):** a toggle beside "Show upcoming", only for an accountant with people under her, adds their checklists, faded and tagged with whose (read-only cards, server-enforced). With no supervisor field, "under her" means **the people doing live work on the clients she can see** (whoever holds checklists or recurring tasks there), as in the Clients list's open/late counts and Completed tasks tab. **Owners are always excluded** (from the toggle, the board and the completed history). It is NOT the client's hand-picked team list (which gates invoices and money) and never widens it.
- **Filter by client:** a multi-select toolbar dropdown narrows the board to the chosen clients ("Clear" = all); it lists only clients with board work and hides itself for ≤1.
- **Which column a checklist lands in:** its "Board column". Each step of a repeating checklist has its own picker (default "Same as recipe" = the recipe's "Default board column"), so a Review step can land elsewhere than Step 1; a one-time task has one. A generated task copies its step's column, else the recipe's, else "Uncategorized". The assistant and To 100% name each step lacking both; it still generates.
- **Column groups + "Hide empty columns":** a column can have a group (e.g. Year-End, Core Accounting). An overview strip above the board shows a chip per group counting its clients with open work (click to jump), each group gets a header, and ungrouped columns sit under "Other". **"Hide empty columns" is ON by default** (remembered per user and device): empty columns and groups are hidden (a group's chip stays, at 0); untick to see all.
- **Changing a column later:** tasks that already have one keep it (a step's new column files the NEXT tasks); a task with none follows its step's current column, else its recipe's. A checklist you moved by hand is never pulled back.
- **Re-tagging an existing checklist:** its Edit (⋯ menu on its card, board or Checklists page) -> "Board column" (incl. "Uncategorized"); the board updates at once. Owner/creator edits apply directly; others' go to the task's approver as any task-details edit.
- **Managing columns (owner only):** "Manage columns": add, rename, reorder or delete columns and set each one's Group (type or pick; empty = none). Deleting a column moves its checklists to "Uncategorized" until re-tagged; a step that used it falls back to its recipe's default.

## Delayed page (owner + staff)

- Every OPEN step flagged "waiting on", grouped by client. Staff see it scoped to their assigned clients. Clear the flag from the Checklists page (or under the client) once unblocked.
- **The list is yours, not the whole firm's**: waits that are your move or that hold you up - the ones you are being waited on for, plus the ones you asked for or own the step for. Marking your part done moves it off your list onto the asker's to confirm; confirming takes it off theirs. This applies to owners too, so there is no firm-wide "everything stuck anywhere" view on this page.
- **Leaving this page is not disappearing.** A confirmed wait drops off everyone's Delayed list and stays on its checklist step as a completed sub-item with the full record: this page is what's still stuck; the step keeps the history.
- An older free-text wait with nobody attached still shows to the step's assignee (or to everyone if the step has none), so nothing predating the two-step hand-off silently vanishes from the page.
- **Two tabs: "Waiting on me" and "I'm waiting on others."** Same underline tab bar as Time Approvals, with a live count in each label; the page opens on whichever has work (clicking the quiet one sticks - it won't bounce back).
  - **Waiting on me** - someone is blocked on you. Each wait has two buttons. **Done** says "my part is finished": the wait goes back to whoever asked, who has the final say; it does NOT tick the checklist step off. **Question** finishes nothing, so the item stays here and stays yours.
  - **I'm waiting on others** - waits you asked for (and waits on a client). Until the other person finishes, the row is a **read-only reminder**: no Done, no buttons, just what you're waiting on and why. Once they mark it done the row gains exactly two buttons, **Approve** and **Send back**.
  - One step can appear on both tabs at once with different waits on each - being owed something and owing something on the same step is normal.
- **Send back ("not approved")** works as described under Checklists; you lose the two buttons until they re-report it finished.
- The step's own button survives on this page only for an OLD free-text wait, which has no wait record to resolve; it is now **Clear wait** and only clears the wait (flag and note), so the step stays open to check off on the Checklists page. A completed step drops off the list (done steps aren't shown).

- Assigned team is the list an OWNER picks: who works this account. Only an owner changes it, on the client's page or the Team page - being given a task on a client does not add anyone to it. **This list is what decides who sees the client's invoices.**
- Seeing a client is wider than being on its team, on purpose: anyone assigned a checklist, recurring template or template stage on a client sees its tasks and logs time to it, on the team or not. Money is the one thing that follows the team alone.
- An owner can be listed on a client's assigned team too - it records who works the account but grants nothing extra: owners already see and act on every client.

## Client Recap (owner only)

- A per-client review page (sidebar: "Client Recap") with a **Monthly / Quarterly / Yearly** toggle and prev/next period navigation - the arrows step by whichever one is active, so Yearly moves 2026 -> 2025. A quarter is three calendar months, a year is the calendar year (Jan 1 - Dec 31); nothing is fiscal or prorated.
- **Download CSV** (beside the period controls) exports the recap for the client and period showing, plus the per-person hours, cost rates and sales-tax status behind it, as one file in a Section / Row / Field / Value layout a spreadsheet can filter or pivot: Client, Period, Billing, Time & hours (per role, with cost and service value), Hours by person, Profitability, Projected invoice, Tasks (one row per task and due date), Sales tax. Money and hours are plain numbers; a blank is the page's em dash; a staff export carries no money sections. Named client-recap-<client>-<period>.csv.
- **The client picker lists active clients by default**. An "Include inactive clients" checkbox beside it puts them back, each marked "(inactive)", their recaps loading as always. Unticking it while on a retired client returns you to the first active one.
- **The page reads plan against reality, top to bottom:** Time & hours, Billing, Profitability, the projected invoice, and **Tasks & workflow last**.
- **The header names the client's billing type** - "Billing type: Hourly" / "Monthly subscription" / "Annual" - right under the page title. A billing master shows none: it has no mode of its own.
- **Time & hours is a table: ESTIMATE | ACTUAL | OVER/UNDER for hours, then COST ESTIMATE | COST ACTUAL | COST OVER/UNDER in dollars, one row per role, with a Total row.** The cost columns price the same rows (a role's estimated hours at its people's pay rate against actual labor cost), colored like hours (under plan is green). No estimate, or no pay rate on file, shows an em dash, never a variance against zero; the labor-cost basis line prints under the table. Rows are per ROLE because estimates are set per role (Client page -> Estimated monthly hours, per CFO / Accountant / Bookkeeper); a row names everyone who filled the role.
- **Everything in that table adds up by hand.** A role's Actual is its people's printed x.xx hours added together; the Total is the roles added together (the same figure as the client's total hours everywhere else); every Over/Under is that row's Actual minus its Estimate. When every role has an estimate, the Total's Over/Under is exactly the rows' Over/Unders added up. A role with hours but **no** estimate (em-dashed) still counts toward the Total - unplanned work is still over plan - and a line under the table says so.
- **Profitability is the same three columns, for profit:** Estimate | Actual | Over/Under, one row. There are no realized-rate or margin tiles; **margin is the Actual column** (revenue - labor cost), beside the plan it is judged against. Definitions and the labor-cost basis print underneath.
- **Billing is three tiles: Service Value | Invoice | Over/Under**. Service Value is the hourly invoice the period's BILLABLE hours would have produced (each person at their own bill rate, each month at its pin, as invoicing prices them), so an hourly client's equals its Invoice; Invoice is the period's service revenue as invoicing prices it (ad hoc at its default, draft edits not reflected), the figure Profitability measures against; Over/Under = Service Value - Invoice, positive meaning the work was worth more than was billed (red; under is the good direction; an exact match says "Matches invoice"). A caption states the definitions. Reimbursements are excluded from both sides and keep their own line and list below. The estimated service revenue stays in Profitability's estimated-profit caption.
- **Ad hoc work counts toward the period's revenue at its default value** - what invoicing it would charge. The recap is computed from the time itself, not a particular draft invoice, so it can't see a "show detail only" or "leave off" set on one month's invoice. It answers "what is this client's work worth", not "what was billed".
- **Labor cost counts team members who have a pay rate on file, owners included; time from anyone without a rate carries no hourly cost**, as in the payroll report and every other cost figure. **Margin is always a figure** - never withheld because someone who logged time has no cost rate. An owner with a Cost rate on the Team page is costed like anybody else; one who leaves it blank costs nothing, so a client she works alone shows its full fee as profit. The reason is printed under every cost and profit figure.
- **Every hours figure on the recap reads as x.xx** - totals and each person's row - and those printed per-person hours are what labor cost is priced from.
- **The role table is in a FIXED order:** CFO tier, then Accountant, then Bookkeeper, then anyone whose role isn't set; people inside a row read alphabetically. The tier comes from the staff role (**Owner -> CFO**, there is no separate CFO role; it matches the client's estimated CFO hours field). A role with no time AND no estimate doesn't appear; a role estimated but never worked appears at 0.00h actual, which is how "planned eight CFO hours, did none" gets caught.
- **Revenue here is the same number the invoice bills** - Recap and the invoice come from one shared calculator. An hourly client's time is valued at each team member's own bill rate, as invoices have charged since June 2026. Monthly and annual clients are unaffected. A quarter or a year is summed month by month, not estimated as a rate times three or twelve.
- **Estimated vs. actual** - the comparison that catches an overrun while it is still happening - has **no separate "Estimated vs. actual" panel**: hours live in Time & hours and profit in Profitability.
  - **Hours per role:** Estimated monthly hours against hours worked, over or under - a Bookkeeper estimated at 10 hours who worked 12 reads "+2.00h over". A **quarterly** recap multiplies the monthly estimates by 3 and a **yearly** one by 12, and the column header says which ("monthly estimates x 12 months").
  - **Profit** (owner only). Estimated profit = expected revenue - estimated cost (each role's estimated hours x that role's cost rate), where expected revenue is the client's monthly rate (monthly clients), a twelfth of the annual fee (annual clients), or the estimated hours at each role's bill rate (hourly clients). Actual profit = invoiced service revenue minus actual labor cost - the Billing and Profitability panels' figures, from the same calculators (reimbursements excluded from both sides), all stated on screen under the figures.
  - **A role's cost/bill rate** comes from the people ASSIGNED to the client in that role, failing that from whoever logged time in it; several people at different rates are averaged, and the panel says so. A role whose people have no pay rate costs nothing on BOTH sides - it never makes the comparison unavailable. That is typically the owner's CFO role, but only while she leaves her Cost rate blank; once set, it prices like any other.
  - **"No estimate set" is a normal, honest state** - most clients have none. They show the actual side only and **no variance**: nothing is compared against a zero nobody entered. A slim banner at the top of Time & hours says so and points at Client page -> Estimated monthly hours.
- **Projected end-of-month invoice** (owner only, monthly view only) - always labeled an Estimate, with its basis printed underneath:
  - A monthly/annual (plan) client: the known, fixed plan amount plus the reimbursements recorded so far.
  - An hourly client: the billable work booked so far scaled by the business days elapsed - "projected from 12.00 billable hours over 10 of 21 business days" - plus reimbursements as recorded. Reimbursements are never extrapolated.
  - A month that has already ended shows the **actual** invoice, described as such, not a projection.
  - Quarterly and Yearly views show no projection: it is a month-shaped question.
- The recap's MONEY - billing, profitability, estimated profit and the projection - is **owner-only**: absent from a staff member's data, not just hidden. **So are the estimates** (planning data set on the Client page, which staff do not manage): a staff payload gets the same role rows and ACTUAL hours with Estimate and Over/Under em-dashed, and no "set them on the Client page" prompt (a dead end for staff). The page is owner-only anyway ("Client Recap" is in owners' sidebars only); the gate is defense in depth.
- **An HOURLY client's quarterly or yearly recap reconciles.** Each month is priced at the **rate month the client was on at the time** (its rate-month history), so a client moved to current rates in March has January and February at the old rates and the figure matches the invoices issued (a month before June 2026 prices as its invoice did, at the client's single hourly rate). Labor cost uses each person's cost rate on the day worked. A **Monthly or Annual** client has one live fee and plan list, so its multi-month revenue is a restatement at today's numbers; the Billing panel says which applies when the period spans more than one month.
- **Sales tax stays monthly or quarterly.** The recap's sales-tax status is month-shaped (it reports one filing task), and recording sales-tax figures against a whole year is refused by the server.

## Reports (owner only)

- Payroll hours report: total hours worked per team member over a WEEKLY or BI-WEEKLY period (toggle), independent of the billing month - for running payroll. Both use the app's Sun-Sat weeks (the weeks staff submit), so bi-weekly = two consecutive Sun-Sat weeks. A date picker + ‹ › (a full period per step) move the window; "This period" jumps to now. To line bi-weekly up with the firm's payroll cycle, set the start to a day in the pay period's first week - the cadence is then preserved. Table of each member's hours (billable/internal split + entry count) with a grand total.
- **Hours read as x.xx** on the summary and the day-and-job detail alike, never one decimal.
- **Every hours TOTAL is the sum of the rows shown above it**, not a rounding of the minutes behind them, so adding the Hours column by hand lands on the printed total: rows of 0.17h + 0.17h + 0.75h total 1.09h, though the underlying 10 + 10 + 45 minutes would round to 1.08h. This composes down the detail table (each day subtotal sums its rows; the grand total sums the day subtotals). Same rule on the Client Recap card (total and billable sum the by-staff list, and Total - vs-prior lands exactly on the printed delta) and in the assistant's hours summaries.
- **Hours is the costing column.** Cost is that figure x the pay rate - checkable by hand, except across a cost-rate change inside the period. There is no exact "Minutes" column.
- **Billable time, Billable $ and Cost appear on the PRINTED report**, not just on screen - on both the per-member summary and the day-and-job detail, with totals.
  - **Billable $** = billable hours x that person's BILL rate - what the work bills at (revenue). Each hour is priced at the person's rate for the RATE MONTH of the client it was worked for - the same figure that client's invoice carries - so a client still on older rates bills at those here; a raise dated ahead reaches a client only once it is moved. The per-person figure adds up the rows at each rate, and the detail rows below split it, so the column ties.
  - **Cost** = ALL hours worked x that person's COST rate - what the firm pays for the time. It covers internal hours too - the firm pays for those as well. Set a person's cost rate on the Team page ("$/hour - for margin reports only, never billed").
  - Side by side, the two columns give margin per person and per day.
  - **An OWNER's Cost is whatever her own cost rate says** (Team page, like everyone else): with one set she is costed from it here. One who leaves it blank shows "-" and adds nothing (a finished answer, not a missing setting); the Cost total is then the firm's STAFF labor cost.
  - Anyone with no rate configured shows "-" rather than "$0.00", so an unset rate never reads as "billed nothing" or "cost nothing"; they contribute nothing to any cost total - a correct, finished state, not a gap.
- **HOW BILLABLE HOURS ARE PRICED ON INVOICES (from 2026-09-01).** An hourly line charges exactly the two-decimal hours it prints, times the rate: "0.05h at $125.00/hr" is $6.25, to the penny, every time. A person's billed hours are the sum of their entries' two-decimal hours - the same figure the Client Recap's roles table and the payroll report show - so the recap's Invoice figure matches its own hours table. Ad hoc lines follow the same rule. Only newly generated invoices price this way: already-issued ones are untouched, and lines from before the change keep their stored amounts even when edited.
- **THE HOURS ON AN HOURLY LINE ARE EDITABLE, AND THE AMOUNT FOLLOWS.** In the month run's editor each hourly line has "Hours" and "Rate" boxes. Changing the hours (1.31 to 1.5) recalculates the amount as hours x rate; changing the rate (a $90/hr client whose line came over at $125) updates the amount and printed detail; a typed rate survives re-tagging time in the hours panel, even after retyping the line's name (each generated hours line remembers whose hours it holds). The amount box is read-only on these lines; the invoice prints the hours you chose; NO automatic rounding. Under the hours the editor always offers the three role rows (CFO / Advisory Services, Accounting Services, Bookkeeping Services), each with an empty row and an "Add hours line" link, so added hours print in the hours section, not under Reimbursed Expenses. A 0-hour row is left off the client's copy; an untouched empty row is not on the invoice.
- **HOW LABOR COST IS CALCULATED - the rule, on every surface. Labor cost = the two-decimal hours shown x the cost rate: round the hours FIRST, then multiply.** So 20h 13.4m at a $16 cost rate is 20.22 x 16 = **$323.52**.
  - **Per person, per period, off the ROWS the report prints.** Round EACH of that person's entries for the period to two-decimal hours and add them up - that sum is the report's Hours figure - then multiply by their cost rate and settle the cent. Across a cost-rate change inside the period, each side's rows are summed and priced at their own rate and the two amounts added. A cost TOTAL is the sum of the per-person amounts.
  - **Why each row and not the period's raw minutes:** the two are not the same number. The figure on the page is the one that gets multiplied.
  - Consequences: **multiplying a visible Hours cell by the pay rate gives the Cost cell beside it**, and **adding up the visible Cost column lands exactly on the total printed underneath it.** A line under the summary totals says so. The one exception to the first: for a person whose cost rate changed inside the period, a single Hours x rate check on their summary row will not match; their day-level rows, which each carry one rate, do.
  - It is the same rule EVERYWHERE - payroll Hours report (summary, detail and all three exports), the Employee report, the Client Recap's labor cost and margin, estimated-vs-actual cost, and the assistant's client profitability and margin answers. They all call the same calculator (which RATE a figure uses is covered below).
  - **BILLABLE $ follows the identical rule**, with the bill rate in place of the cost rate: the billable-hours figure shown x that person's bill rate. Multiply the Billable hours cell by hand and you get the Billable $ cell. Per-entry Billable $ cells are that period total split across the person's own rows, as the Cost cells are.
  - **It changed on 2026-08-19 and was corrected again on 2026-08-26.** Before 08-19, cost came from exact seconds rounded once at the end, so the printed Hours column did not multiply out (reports carried an exact-minutes column to explain a few cents of drift); 08-19 changed the rule, it fixed nothing. On 08-26 the money, still priced off the period's re-rounded raw minutes, moved to the sum of the rows, so Cost, Billable $ and the hours beside them come from one figure on the payroll summary, the day-and-job detail, the Employee report and every export. Each change moved figures a few cents to a couple of dimes per person per period, either way. Nothing stored changed either time: every report, past and present, recomputes under the current rule.
  - **Per-ENTRY Cost cells** in the day-and-job detail table and the "Raw hours" export are that person's pay for the PERIOD split across their own entries, so **the Cost column adds up to the total under it exactly**. A row lands within a few cents of its hours x rate, closer the more entries there are; a muted note under the detail totals says so on the page. A full-mode repeat shows no cost, on screen and in the export alike (the firm pays for the block once).
  - **WHICH cost rate, now that rates are dated.** Every cost figure on the payroll and Employee reports - per-entry Cost cells and the "Raw hours" export, each person's PERIOD Cost (the payroll summary's Cost column, the Summary CSV, the Employee report's Cost and its CSV), and every total under them - uses the cost rate the person was paid on the DAY each entry was worked. So the summary, the detail and their totals always agree: a raise mid-period shows up only in the rows it landed on, and hours worked before it are never paid at the new rate.
- Payroll report detail - "Time by day and job": below the per-member summary, the same period day by day. **EVERY TIME ENTRY IS LISTED INDIVIDUALLY** - never merged for sharing a day, client or task. Each row shows the job (the client billed; '(Admin)' for non-client time), team member, task, CLOCK IN, CLOCK OUT, session count, hours, billable hours, Billable $ and Cost. Rows sit under their day's header with that day's total, in clock-in order (minutes- only entries, with no timestamps, come last with "-" for the stamps). A "Team member" filter scopes the detail to one person (payroll is usually run per person) or all.
- **Group splits made in "full" mode count ONCE toward payroll hours and cost.** Full mode bills every client on the split the WHOLE block (a 1-hour block across 3 clients is 3 billable hours - the intended billing), but the person worked, and the firm pays for, one hour, so tracked hours, day subtotals, grand totals and Cost count the block once. Every slice still appears as its own row and counts toward Billable / Billable $; the repeated rows carry a muted "full block · counted once" note (and no Cost). Even and custom splits carve the block up and count normally.
- Payroll exports (three): "Summary CSV" (per-member totals), "By day & job (summary)" (Date, Team member, Job, Task, Hours, Billable hours - the COLLAPSED breakdown, ready to pivot; the on-screen table is per-entry), and "Raw hours" (one row per time entry, scoped to the pay period and member filter). Both RAW exports carry "Billable hours" and "Billable $", and payroll "Raw hours" also "Cost" - matching the printed report, blank rather than 0.00 when the person has no rate set.
- **The payroll exports price Cost off the Hours column they carry**. "Summary CSV" is Employee, Tracked hours, Billable hours, Internal hours, Entries, Cost - the original five keep their names and positions, with Cost appended. The "Tracked minutes (exact)" / "Tracked hours (4dp)" columns (and "Minutes (exact)" / "Hours (4dp)" on "Raw hours") were REMOVED on 2026-08-19.
- Both RAW exports (payroll "Raw hours" and the monthly "Hours by month") include CLOCK IN and CLOCK OUT stamps plus a Sessions count, for auditing hours against when the work happened: clock in = the first start, clock out = the last stop, and Sessions > 1 marks a day split across several stretches. Entries logged as minutes only (no timer/manual timestamps) leave them blank.
- Month summary: tracked hours, internal hours, billable mix, projected billing (each hourly client priced at its pinned rate month, as its invoice would be), employee coverage.
- Employee report (hours by person, with **Billable $** and **Cost**, priced exactly as on the payroll report; owners included) and Client report (hours by client), each with Download CSV. Print-friendly; the Cost column is on the printed employee table and its CSV, with the payroll tables' "-" (never "$0.00") no-rate rule.
- Hours by month: a raw CSV of every time entry in the selected period (Date, Employee, Client, Task, Hours, Billable, Description), sorted by date - for month-by-month detail / external bookkeeping.

## Invoices (owner only)

> **The monthly run is live.** At the top of the Invoices page: pick a month, press **Generate**, and the app builds one numbered draft invoice per client with something to bill - lines, a due date, any prior-month adjustment carried forward, and out-of-scope flags.
>
> **The client is asked to pay on receipt; the due date the app records is the firm's own.** The invoice and email say **"Due on receipt"** with no date. The app stores **30 days after the invoice is first SENT** - the firm's internal past-due line, after which it is late and chased - shown only in the month run as "Due on receipt · follow up Oct 31" (hovering says so), never to the client. Generate stamps a **provisional** date (30 days from building the draft); the first send replaces it with 30 days from the day the email went out. **Sending AGAIN never moves the line**.
>
> **The exception: a client whose own payment terms name a LONGER window.** "Net 45" on the record means the invoice prints their own wording and due date - the window they were promised - and the first send re-stamps 45 days from sending, not 30. Shorter terms ("Net 15"), pay-now terms ("Due on receipt", "Due on Demand") or blank terms all produce a pay-on-receipt invoice chased after 30 days.
>
> **Moving between months:** back/forward arrows flank the month picker - one press steps one month, no calendar popup; the picker still jumps to a distant month. Stepping with unsaved edits in an open invoice asks before discarding them, as the picker does.
>
> **Status tabs** - **To review**, **Reviewed**, **Sent**, **Processing**, **Past due**, **Payment failed**, **Paid**, **Voided**. A bank payment still clearing (about 4 business days) sits under **Processing**; the row says 'Bank payment clearing since <date>'. **Past due** and **Payment failed** are DERIVED tabs, not statuses: an invoice in either is still Sent and still owed, worked out fresh on each draw. (No "Overdue" status is ever written: being late is a fact about today's date.) All eight tabs show counts; an empty one is dimmed but still there.
>
> **Past due** is the chase list: sent, still owed, past the 30-day line. The row carries a red **Past due · N days** flag (hovering names the line: "Past-due line was <date>"). Due TODAY is not past due (the client has the day). Opening one: **Send reminder** or **Send again** to nudge (the line does not move, so it stays until paid), or **Mark paid** if settled another way. One both past due AND payment-failed shows under **Payment failed** only.
>
> **Payment failed** is the follow-up list: a payment did not go through - usually a hand-entered bank account not verified within Stripe's 10-day microdeposit window, or a returned debit. Still **Sent** and owed (nothing collected). The row shows a red **Payment failed [date]** flag; opening it shows Stripe's reason; the client's permanent Pay link still works for a retry, so follow up: **Send again** (same link) or **Send reminder** moves it back to Sent, or **Mark paid** if they pay another way. Owners are notified at once (bell + email) with invoice, client and reason.
>
> **A payment for a different amount than the invoice total** (paid on a payment page opened before the invoice changed) is recorded as usual and flagged under **Need a look** until an owner marks it handled: the row shows an amber **Paid $X, total $Y** flag (even on a Paid invoice) and opening it says what was paid against that total - bill or refund the difference, then press **Mark as handled** (owners only). Every owner is notified once (bell + email) with both amounts. While a bank payment is processing the notice says so (the row reads **Paying**), and the flag clears itself if that payment fails. With several payments waiting, the notice and notification say how many (the newest shown), and the notification links to that month. Mark as handled clears them all.
>
> **A second payment on an invoice already Paid** (bank then card, or twice) is not added: the invoice stays Paid, flagged amber under **Need a look** (**Second payment $X**: the money is in Stripe, not applied) until an owner refunds or applies it by hand and presses **Mark as handled**. **Apply as credit** instead adds it to the paying client's credit on account (a billing sub's master): what reached the firm (a card payment's fee comes off; raisable to the full charge), never a refunded or disputed payment; it asks Stripe first (a bank payment still settling is refused) and clears only that payment's flag. If Stripe reports it refunded or disputed, its ledger row says so (a closed dispute shows its result): Void is one click while unspent, a used one is handled by hand, and such a credit is not drawn onto invoices automatically (Apply still can) unless its dispute was won and it was never refunded; nothing is voided automatically. **A payment for a voided invoice** is noted on its history (it stays Void) and every owner is notified once (client, invoice number, amount): refund or re-apply the money by hand.
>
> Within a tab invoices are **alphabetical by client by default**; a Sort control offers invoice-number order or total (high to low); rows never rearrange while you work. Reviewing, sending or voiding one moves it out of the tab you are on (its new tab's count goes up; you stay on your tab).
>
> **Search the month:** a box above the tabs narrows every tab to invoices matching a client name or invoice number ("N of M" count). The stat strip stays whole-month, and an invoice open for editing never disappears because of what was typed. History has the same box, composing with its Year / Client / Status filters; its month tables also open alphabetical by client (click a column header to re-sort).
>
> Above the tabs: counts for To review / Reviewed / Need a look / Past due and the month total (Past due turns amber when non-empty). Rows flagged for a second look get an amber rule down the left and keep their number-order place, so they turn up in any tab (an amber dot on a tab means some are in there). Switching tabs with unsaved edits asks first.
>
> **AI confidence rating (advisory).** After Generate, an AI reviewer rates each monthly draft with a row badge: **high confidence**, **check N things**, or **low confidence**. It checks the arithmetic (printed hours x each person's bill rate, reconciled against the month's tracked time), plan-vs-hourly consistency, covered-date windows, ad hoc dispositions, month names in descriptions, and last month's comparison. The open invoice shows its summary, specific concerns and up to three questions for you. THE RATING IS ADVISORY ONLY - IT NEVER BLOCKS, CHANGES, SENDS OR TOUCHES THE NUMBERS; unrated or low-confidence invoices go as before. **Re-rate** refreshes it after edits (the card says if you edited since). Retainer invoices are not rated (one manual line); older invoices have no badge.
>
> **The AI's questions (skippable).** **Mark reviewed** on an invoice with unanswered questions shows them again with answer boxes - **Answer & approve** or **Skip & approve**; approval is never held up. Answers are remembered and sharpen future ratings; skipping costs nothing. They can also be answered any time from the invoice.
>
> Click any row to edit: a line's wording or amount, add or remove lines, the note to the client (**This invoice only**, or **Keep for future invoices** so every new invoice starts with it), then **Print** or **Mark reviewed**. **Preview** opens what the client receives (the email and its PDF), built by Send's code, on any invoice that can still go out, drafts included; it names the recipients, says when dates show as if sent today, that Send asks first on a held billing-period month, and that the Pay button's address is made at send. It sends and saves nothing and waits behind unsaved edits, like Print (stored lines, as the per-client view prints them). Reviewed can go **Back to draft**. **Void** keeps the invoice on record, struck through, closes the client's open payment page and frees the client to be generated again; it asks first, saying what it means for a sent or paid invoice (a paid one is not refunded). One with a bank payment still clearing cannot be voided until it is paid or fails (Void & regenerate skips it too).
>
> **What the client's invoice looks like.** Printed invoice, emailed PDF and email body share one layout: titled sections, each ending "Total <section>" - **Subscription Plan**, **Ad-Hoc / Billable Hours** (total reads "Total Ad-Hoc/Billable Hours", no spaces around the slash), **Client Reimbursed Expenses**, **Software** (recurring expenses ticked Software) - then the Total due. Empty sections are left off (an hourly client has no Subscription Plan section), as is a $0 monthly service line on the client's copy. Hours lines group by who worked - **CFO / Advisory Services** (owner), **Accounting Services** (accountant), **Bookkeeping Services** (bookkeeper) - with hours, rate and amount. Header: **Invoice no.**, **Invoice Date** (monthly invoices from September 2026: last day of the billing month, never later than the day issued; retainers and earlier months: the day issued; the follow-up line counts from the send day), **Billing Period: Month Year**. No firm tagline in the letterhead; closing line "Spread success, not stress, thanks for choosing PB&J Strategic Accounting." unless the client has its own footer note. **Time breakdown** on (client setting): detailed hours print on a separate **page 2**, "Detailed Hours" (default off: one page). A billing master's combined invoice is unaffected (see Combined billing). Payment-terms line and **Due** follow the due-date rule above.

> **MARK PAID - for money that arrived outside the app** (a check, a transfer nobody linked, an invoice never sent through the system): open it in the month run and press **Mark paid**. Works on Draft, Reviewed and Sent (a past-due one is still Sent); NOT offered while a bank payment is **going through** (a real debit is settling; the two would race). It records the date, locks the invoice like any paid one, and **kills any payment links already emailed for it** so a client cannot pay twice with an old one. Who marked it and when is kept on the record.

It asks Stripe whether it settled and records only Stripe's answer: settled moves it to Paid with the real charge time; still-settling changes nothing and the button says so. It fixes a payment completed on Stripe that never flipped here (a lost or out-of-order webhook), never overriding a live payment.

> **Verify all with Stripe** - the same check as one sweep, in the month run's action row (next to Download for QBO): EVERY invoice in any month still going through is checked independently against Stripe in one press, recording only its answers. It then says how many confirmed paid (named by number), how many still settling, how many could not be checked; with nothing holding it says so. Use it when payments look stuck.

> **Undo manual payment** appears only on invoices marked paid BY HAND - it returns the invoice to Sent (or Reviewed if never sent), editable and collectible again. An invoice a real payment settled has no such button: money that moved stays recorded as it moved.

> **ONCE AN INVOICE IS PAID IT IS LOCKED**, so it keeps matching what the client paid: lines and note go read-only, **Save changes**, **Add a line** and the remove buttons are gone, and a gray line at the top says why. The same lock applies while a payment is **going through** (an ACH debit takes days to settle). Nothing lifts it, not even **Back to draft**. **Void is the way out:** void a wrong paid invoice and issue a new one (the void stays on the record, visible to you and the client). Invoices **sent but not paid** stay fully editable. The lock also keeps a **retainer credit** honest: only a paid retainer can be credited, and a paid total cannot change.

> **AD HOC WORK IS SHOWN SEPARATELY, AND YOU DECIDE WHAT TO DO WITH EACH PIECE.** Time flagged "Ad hoc" on the Time page does not disappear into that person's hours line: it gets its own block on the draft, headed **"Ad hoc - outside scope"**, one line per piece of work - `Adhoc - <what was done>`, with the day, who did it, and the hours at their rate underneath. Each line's dropdown has three answers:
>
> - **Invoice it** (default) - an ordinary charge at that person's rate; the amount can be overtyped like any line.
> - **Show detail only ($0.00)** - the line appears on the client's invoice at no charge. No reason asked.
> - **Leave off the invoice** - the client never sees it. It stays on your draft at $0.00 so you can put it back; changing your mind restores the amount it was holding.
>
> On the two not charging, the row reads $0.00 (what the client sees) with **"would be $X"** beside the dropdown. The choice flows through the month total, printed invoice, PDF and email - all read the same lines. Courtesy lines print at $0.00; omitted lines are not printed (and are left out of the QBO export - a courtesy line is exported, since it IS on their invoice).
>
> **A piece of time is billed exactly once.** An ad hoc entry is either an ad hoc line or part of the ordinary hours - never both.
>
> Ad hoc applies to **hourly clients** from June 2026 onward. Flat-fee (subscription / annual) clients are unaffected for now: their billable hours are covered by the fee, so flagged time is recorded but not charged. Regenerating a month rebuilds the ad hoc lines from current data, discarding the choices made on them like any other edit.
>
> **THE HOURS BEHIND THE INVOICE SIT BESIDE IT, AND YOU CAN RE-TAG THEM THERE.** An invoice opened in the month run shows an **Hours this period** panel beside the lines (below on a narrow screen): every time entry for its month and client, by team member, with Date, Description, Task and Hours; each member's heading carries in-scope, ad hoc and out-of-scope subtotals. On a billing master rows group by company, then person. Each row's dropdown - **In scope**, **Out of scope**, **Ad hoc** - shows the tag the entry ALREADY has from logging or review. **Ad hoc** offers the ad hoc block's three options; billed ad hoc work is charged at that person's bill rate for the client's rate month (a billing master: each company's own), exactly as Generate priced the hours beside it.
>
> **Nothing happens until you press Save.** Tags stage up, marked **"Will apply on save"**, the running total under the lines showing what the invoice will come to. One Save writes the lines and re-tagged entries together; until then Print, Send and the payment link wait behind "Save your changes first". Re-tagging never changes an entry's approval. **Out of scope travels beyond this invoice:** it marks the time non-billable everywhere, reports and analytics included; to keep the hours billable but off this one invoice, use **Ad hoc -> Leave off the invoice**.
>
> **When the lines have been renamed, the tag saves but the money does not move.** The panel shifts hours only between lines carrying their own hours and rate (the per-person hours lines Generate writes, which remember whose hours they hold), so retitling one ("CFO/Advisory Services") is fine. On a line without them (a draft generated before that, once its name is retyped, or one typed by hand: "For services rendered for the month of") nothing says which line holds an entry, so the app will not add a charge it cannot also subtract: the row says so (*"The tag stands, but these hours are on a renamed line - adjust the invoice line yourself"*), the tag is recorded, you edit the line by hand, and **that warning stays on the row after the save** while lines and tag disagree. The same refusal covers a line rounded DOWN below the hours leaving it (left exactly as typed, not deleted) and **an ad hoc row whose own line was renamed** (the panel cannot re-price it or change its mode).
>
> The panel does not offer the decision for **subscription and annual clients** or **months before June 2026** (no per-employee hourly lines): hours show for reference, controls disabled, with a line saying why - and a tag sent for one of those anyway (a stale tab, a replayed request) is refused by the server, not half-applied. So is a re-tag on **sent, processing, paid and voided** invoices (past review the client holds a copy of what it would change). A month signed off by a timesheet lock shows a **Month locked** note on the row; an owner may still re-tag it.

> **Download for QBO** exports the month as a line-level CSV for QuickBooks' invoice import. Voided invoices and retainers recorded as paid outside the app are left out. ⚠️ The `Item` column is a placeholder until Brittany confirms the product/service names in her own QBO file - everything else in the export is exact.

A client with nothing to bill gets no invoice. **Nor does a client opted out of platform invoicing**, and the run says so when it finishes ("… 1 client opted out of platform invoicing."); see **Opt out** below.
>
> **Void & regenerate** (next to Generate). Generate leaves an existing invoice alone; this button voids every **Draft** and **Reviewed** invoice for the chosen month and immediately rebuilds them from current data. It NEVER touches an invoice that has gone out (Sent - past due included - Processing, Paid) or any other month. The confirm gives the actual counts; line edits, the note to the client and the review status on the voided invoices are **discarded**. The button is grayed out with no unsent invoices. Afterwards it reports how many were voided, rebuilt, and left alone (sent or paid).
>
> **Expect the invoice numbers to jump after a regenerate - that is fine.** Numbers run `INV-2026-08-001`, `INV-2026-08-002`, … within a month. A voided invoice KEEPS its number and numbers are never reused, so rebuilt invoices get fresh higher ones. **Download for QBO** leaves voided invoices out.

> **Stripe is LIVE - real money moves.** Payment links and card payments run on the live Stripe account, and real payments have settled through it. A client can pay from the app by bank transfer (ACH) from a payment link, or by card. Since every send and link is real, practice or testing belongs on the **Test** client, never a real one.
>
> **Payment link (bank transfer).** It does NOT open a payment page (Brittany is not the payer); it gives the invoice's secure link to copy to the client. Offered only on a **Sent** invoice (past-due included) with something owed; it does not change the status. ACH takes about **4 business days to clear**, so it reads **Processing** until **Paid**. A failed payment puts the invoice back to Sent under **Payment failed** (above).
>
> **The payment link does not expire.** It is a PB&J address (`app.pbjsa.com/pay/...`), not a Stripe one, and builds a fresh Stripe payment page each time it is opened, so a link emailed weeks ago still works. It is the emailed invoice's (and reminder's) **Pay** link - one per invoice; pressing **Payment link** again hands back the same one. Opened after the invoice is settled or withdrawn it charges nobody twice: a plain page says it is already paid, already being processed, or was canceled. Why an old email's link stopped working: its button carried a Stripe address, which expires after about a day.
>
> **After the client pays, the payment page thanks them**: after checkout it says the payment is being confirmed with the bank and shows Paid once it clears (never claiming the money arrived, nor sending them into an app they cannot sign into). A client who backs out sees "Nothing was charged" and a button to start again. **A card payer comes back to the card page:** backing out of or finishing on the card link (`app.pbjsa.com/pay/.../card`) returns them to it, so a retry gets the card page they chose, fee shown, not a quiet switch to bank transfer.
>
> **Opening a payment link is written into the invoice's history** as a "Payment link opened" line, at most once a day, however often reloaded. It is NOT a send and never marks the invoice sent. Only a real browser visit is logged; link checkers and mail scanners are not.
>
> **Send (email the invoice).** In the month-run editor, next to Payment link, **Send** emails the invoice to **every address attached to that client**: each linked contact's general address AND any client-specific addresses on that contact (a contact on several clients can hold a different address for each, several for one - all are used), plus the client record's address. Archived contacts are skipped; no address gets two copies. A contact's personal address counts too (some clients get mail that way).
>
> Each row shows its recipient count, opening to names ("Name <address>"); **no address on file** is flagged, Send disabled with the reason. The email carries the full breakdown, total, due date and note to the client, plus a pink **Pay $[amount]** button (bank transfer; the invoice's permanent link) when owed; a re-send of a Paid or Processing invoice is a statement with NO pay button. A paid invoice's email (credit, Stripe or by hand) reads "Paid in full - nothing is owed" with "Paid <date> by <method>" (by hand: "Paid <date>") and no Amount due; **Preview** shows it. Sending marks it **Sent**; the first send's date is THE sent date. The editor shows the last send ("Sent Aug 9 to 2 recipients", opening to the addresses) and the button becomes **Send again**. A draft must be **marked reviewed** first. A failed send shows the provider's error and never marks it sent; every attempt stays in a permanent per-invoice email log with the total billed then.
>
> **Send reminder** (beside Send again; also small on each Sent and Past due row) emails a short reminder: amount due, invoice, client, due wording, the invoice's **Pay** link (card link if on); no attachment. It opens the recipient picker (an address changed since refuses). Off, saying why: not sent yet, bank payment clearing, nothing owed, autopay collecting, Changed since sent (use Send again), reminded or sent today (once a day), or a paid prepayment covers the month; off or absent if the client is not emailed. Row and editor: **Last reminded <date>** ("(automatic)" if the app sent it), or "Sent, but not recorded - do not send again today". **Remind all past due** / **Remind all sent** (those tabs) list recipients (Sent: how long ago each was sent) and skips with reasons, then send one at a time, month held: refused ones are skipped, a send failure stops ("Reminded N of M"). Never changes sent date, status, due date or autopay, but **clears Payment failed** (the confirm says so; it returns to Sent or Past due; the editor keeps a "cleared by a reminder <date>" note). **Automatic:** on the 15th, 20th, 25th and last day of the month (from 9 am firm time) the app sends this same reminder (same rules, once a day) for every unpaid invoice until paid, to the addresses it went to (not if never emailed here, sent under 5 days ago, or those addresses changed), then files a summary per run that did something, naming any Payment failed it cleared; `INVOICE_AUTO_REMINDERS=off` pauses it (`=preview` only says who would be reminded).
>
> **Who an email goes to (the recipient picker).** Every manual client email opens one picker first: invoice **Send**/**Send again**/**Email invoice**, **Send reminder**, **Remind all** (**Change** per invoice), **Letters** (**Change** per client), engagement and proposal **Send**, **Send questionnaire**/**Send again**, **Invite to autopay**. It lists the addresses on file (contacts, client record; proposal/engagement: the prospect or party first, then its client's; a new questionnaire link: none; letters: never a team address) with the usual ones ticked (engagement, proposal: the party). Untick any; **Add another address for this send only** takes up to 3 (valid, not already listed; saved nowhere); one on-file address must stay ticked; only an invoice (**Send to other addresses...** beside Send), engagement or new questionnaire link with none on file can go to added ones alone (autopay: Invite is off with no address). The server refuses addresses not on file; a stale tick is refused (most pickers reopen on the fresh list); logs show the list used. Automatic reminders and receipts are unchanged.

> **If you change an invoice after it was sent**, the open editor says "This invoice was already sent. The client has the earlier version - send it again so they have your changes." with a **Send again** button beside it, and the row carries a quiet **Changed since sent** badge until you send again (only changes the client would see count: lines, covered dates, the note to the client, the total). A changed total closes any payment page the client has open (it cannot be paid at the old amount); the email's Pay button still works and opens a fresh page at the new total.
>
> **You can see whether the email arrived.** Next to "Sent … to …" a badge says what the mail provider did with it:
>
> - **Delivered** - reached the client's mail server. Nothing to do.
> - **Delayed** - the client's server is holding it for a retry; normal, usually clears within the hour. Nothing to do yet.
> - **Bounced** - could not be delivered. **Check the client's email address** on their Contacts (a typo, a closed mailbox, a changed domain), fix it, and press **Send again**. Hover the badge for the provider's reason.
> - **Marked as spam** - it arrived and the client's provider filed it as junk. **Ask the client to add billing@pbjsa.com to their contacts or safe-sender list**, then press **Send again**. Nothing is wrong with the invoice.
>
> **No badge means no news yet** - sent a moment ago, or before this existed. The badge never changes the invoice's status: a bounce does not un-send it, the bill stands and the due date has not moved. **A bounce or a spam complaint also notifies every owner** through the bell.

> **The invoice email is built to stay out of spam.** It comes from the firm's name, not a bare address ("PB&J Strategic Accounting <billing@pbjsa.com>"), replies go to the billing mailbox, one plain sentence before the pay button says what is owed and when, and the footer carries the firm's address, phone and email - the same details as the PDF letterhead. A client's own footer note, if any, also appears in the email body after the totals.

> **Choosing who gets it.** When a client has **more than one** address, Send opens a checkbox list - one line per address, showing whose it is - with **everything ticked**. Untick any you do not want; it goes only to (and the log records) the ticked ones. A single address sends with no dialog. The per-client **Email invoice** button shows the same list. The server only accepts that client's own addresses and cannot be asked to email anyone else.

The quote is also the image's alt text and a plain-text line, and the pay button is a colored button, not an image, so both read with images blocked.

Same content as the printed invoice (logo and firm details, number, month, dates, who it bills with their mailing address, every line, totals, terms, the note to the client and the footer note). If the PDF cannot be built for any reason the email still goes out - without the attachment, never held back.

> **After the client pays, the client hears from us** - automatically; nothing is queued for Brittany to send.

> - **When the payment completes (either channel):** a receipt with the amount, method, date and invoice number - and the invoice **PDF stamped PAID** attached, rebuilt after the payment so the stamp names the date and channel and a card-paid invoice's fee line is in it.
> - **Card payments go straight to paid**, so a card client gets the receipt only, never the acknowledgment.

> **Pay by card - a per-client option, off by default.** A **Pay by card** toggle on a client's **Billing** tab (owner only). **Bank transfer is the no-fee default for everyone and always stays available.** With it on, the emailed invoice keeps **Pay by bank transfer** and adds a smaller card option underneath: "Prefer to pay by card? Pay $103.30 (includes a $3.30 card processing fee - bank transfer has no fee)." Both links are the invoice's permanent PB&J addresses (the card one is the same address ending in /card); each opens a fresh Stripe page, and paying through one **immediately kills the other**.

It is worked backwards from what the firm must net (2.9% + 30¢ on the charged amount), rounded never to be a penny under: on a $100.00 invoice the client pays $103.30 and the firm receives $100.00.

> **Where the fee shows up:** nowhere unless the client pays by card - the invoice, month run and QuickBooks show the ordinary total while unpaid. When a card payment lands, a **Card processing fee** line is added and the total recalculated, so History, the month run and the QBO export show what arrived. A bank-transfer payer is never charged a fee and no fee line appears.

> **The Payment link button stays bank transfer only**, even for a card-enabled client: it hands back a bare URL, and the fee explanation would not travel with it. Card is offered through **Send**, where the email explains it.

>

>

> **Opt out of platform invoicing - a per-client switch, off by default.** On a client's **Billing** tab (owner only), beside Pay by card: "No invoices are generated or sent for this client here; they are billed outside the app." On, **nothing is generated and nothing can go out**: the month run builds no invoice for them, **Generate for this one client** and **Email invoice** answer "<Client> is invoiced outside the app, so no invoice was created", and a retainer invoice is refused the same way. An earlier invoice stays on screen flagged **Opted out**, **Send** grayed out with that reason, as is **Payment link** once sent (not offered before); a pay link already in the client's inbox stops taking money and shows the "cannot be paid online right now" page. Their time, tasks, team and contacts are untouched; the setup checklist stops asking for a rate or email. Switching it off puts them back in the next month run.

> **Never-email switch.** **Invoice delivery** card on the Billing tab (owner only): "Generate the invoice but never email it". The invoice is still built and reviewed; there is no Send or payment link, and **Mark reviewed** marks it sent with no email. Autopay never charges these clients (and its invitation is refused). A pay link already in an inbox opens a page saying the invoice can't be paid online, and the setup checklist stops asking for a billing email.

> **Email invoice can build the invoice first.** The per-client **Email invoice** button sends the STORED invoice for the client and month on screen. With no live invoice for the month (none, or only voided ones) it asks: "<Client> has no invoice for August 2026 yet. Generate it now? (Just this client - nothing else is created.)" Yes builds ONE invoice, for that client only, as a **draft** (it says so) ("Invoice INV-… created as a draft - mark it reviewed in the … month run above, then send"); it does NOT send or review it. No changes nothing. With nothing to bill (no hours, plan or reimbursements that month) it says so instead of creating an empty invoice. An opted-out client gets nothing built (see Opt out above).

> **History - every month, on the same page.** A switch at the very top of Invoices: **This month** (everything above - the run and the per-client view below it - where the page always opens) and **History**, the archive. Not remembered between visits.

Click a heading to sort (again to reverse); sorting is **within** each month, which stay newest first. Each row does two things: **Print** (the same printed invoice, from the stored lines), and **clicking the invoice number opens that month in the run** (the switch flips back to This month and the picker moves to that month). With unsaved edits open in the run it asks first, naming the month holding them ("You have unsaved invoice edits in August 2026"); keeping them leaves you in History with a line saying so. Until months have been generated, History says so and shows nothing.

> **RETAINERS - the two ends of an engagement:** a **retainer invoice** when the client signs, and a **retainer credit** on the invoice you decide is the last one. The monthly invoicing in between is untouched.
>
> **Issuing one, and its position.** Client **Billing** tab, **Retainer invoice** panel. With no retainer yet: type the amount, press **Issue retainer invoice…**; after a confirm it makes a DRAFT retainer invoice. Already invoiced and paid outside the app? Tick "record it only" and pick the Date paid: saved as a paid retainer filed under that date's month, tagged "Recorded outside the app", never emailed, creditable later, not un-markable (void it). Once issued or recorded, the panel shows its position instead: amount, credit applied, remaining balance, and whether it was recorded as paid outside the app. **Increase retainer** opens the form for a second retainer (its own invoice); **Apply** opens the Invoices page, where the credit goes on a draft or reviewed invoice. Signing an engagement letter drafts one too.
>
> **After that it is an ordinary invoice**, in the month run for its issue month and in History, tagged **Retainer**: review, edit, send, take payment and print it like any other, separate from that month's regular invoice. **Generate** and **Void & regenerate** ignore retainers: a retainer never blocks the month's real invoice, and rebuilding a month never throws one away. **Retainer numbers run on their own sequence:** `INV-RET-2026-001`, … counted per YEAR, not per month.
>
> **Giving it back - THE APP OFFERS, YOU DECIDE WHEN.** Once a retainer is **paid**, every invoice for that client shows **Apply retainer credit ($X)** beside "Add a line". Never applied automatically: which invoice ends an engagement is your call. Pressing it adds a negative line **"Retainer applied - credit"**, lowering the total.
>
> - **Applying and removing are both yours.** Delete the credit line (bin icon) and save: the retainer goes back on account, offered again on the next invoice you open.
> - **The credit is added before the invoice goes out.** Apply appears on **Draft** and **Reviewed** invoices only; applied before sending, it travels through Sent and Paid like any other line, and the invoice stays editable.
> - **The credit amount is not typed:** the app sizes it from the retainer and the rest of the invoice on every save (read-only box); the line's wording is yours to change.
> - **Voiding gives the retainer back.** Void a credited invoice (singly or by regenerating the month) and the retainer returns to the account, offered again on the next invoice.
> - **You cannot void a retainer that has been given back.** Refused, saying to remove the credit from that invoice first.
> - **With two retainers, the button names the one it offers**, the older first (no choosing yet: apply it, then the second).
> - **A retainer can never be given back twice.** The app records which invoice took it, enforced on save, not just in the buttons: a second invoice (another tab or owner) is refused ("That retainer has already been applied to another invoice"), nothing stored.
> - **The credit can never make an invoice negative.** A retainer larger than the final invoice stops at its total ($0.00, never below), and the button says how much of it the invoice can take. Applying it settles the retainer in full; any remainder is returned outside the app.
> - **A fully credited invoice still goes out** at $0.00 as a statement, with no pay button; otherwise the pay link and card option (if the client has one) charge the NET amount.
>
> **Where retainers show up elsewhere:** **History** (tagged Retainer) and **Download for QBO** - the retainer invoice and the credit line both export, sharing one item name so they net out over the engagement (a retainer recorded as paid outside the app does not export: its invoice is already in QuickBooks). **Client Recap revenue does NOT count retainers**: held on account, they are recognized through the monthly invoices they offset (crediting the final invoice does not dent that month's revenue).
>
> **Combined billing (billing masters).** A **billing master** does no work itself but bills a group of companies (its "subs"). Each sub keeps its time, checklists, reimbursements and recap but gets no monthly invoice ("Billed on the master's invoice" shows where); the master's monthly invoice carries every sub's charges. The owner sees per-company subtotals in the month run, but THE CLIENT SEES ONE COMBINED LINE ("Bookkeeping services - the month") and one total: no company names or amounts on the printed invoice, PDF or email. The email goes to the sub contacts the owner picked ("Combined invoice recipient", under Billing on the master's client page); with none, sending refuses and points there. Retainers are issued per company, never combined. The master's Client Recap rolls up each company's numbers (each sub keeps its own); sales tax status and projection do not, showing a dash saying why. A billing master refuses its own time, checklists and recurring reimbursements.
>
> **Billing period.** A monthly-rate client can pay every few months (client page -> Billing -> **Billing period**: "Bill every [N] months, starting [month]" ; whole number 1-24, start needed above 1; hourly, Annual, billing masters and their subs ignore it). A period's first invoice also carries a **Prepayment for <Month>** line (current fee) per later month, under a **Prepayment** heading (QuickBooks: **Deferred Revenue**); an anchor invoice without them holds nothing. Once paid, each line is credit on account for its month ("Prepayment on INV-…", no Void) that month's invoice draws (the paid invoice cannot be voided or un-marked while one does). Before a later month goes out (month run Send, lower **Email invoice**, or **Mark reviewed** for a never-email client) it asks if its prepayment is unpaid (**Send anyway** / **Mark reviewed anyway**; flag **Prepayment unpaid**), still clearing (**Prepayment clearing**) or paid but not applied (**Prepayment not applied**); those two have no override: Apply credit on account, or Void & regenerate once a clearing payment settles. A month already at $0 (other credit or a retainer) is never held. Voiding an anchor invoice withdraws its prepayments: later months bill in full until the period's invoice is generated again, and a paid one's credit goes with it. Changing the period or start mid-period warns on the card if a month would be prepaid twice (the extra becomes credit on account).
>
> The per-client section BELOW the run is the older live-calculation view, kept for its preview and print.

- Per-client invoice drafts for the selected billing month: subscription plans and/or billable hours become line items, with the total due. Hourly clients' hours are charged per team member at that person's own bill rate **as it stood in the rate month that client is pinned to**, one line each, titled by role ("CFO / Advisory Services", "Accounting Services" or "Bookkeeping Services"; the name is added when two share a role; anyone else keeps "Billable hours - <name>"). So raising a bill rate does NOT raise every hourly client next month; a client moves to newer rates at its review (Client page -> Billing -> Hourly rates). Which clients a raise reaches, the FIRST-rate exception, and how someone with no bill rate bills: Team -> Bill rate. Per-employee billing applies from June 2026; earlier months keep the client's prior hourly rate. Nothing already sent is ever repriced (a sent invoice carries its own rates). The on-screen previews (this view, Projected billing on Reports and the Dashboard, the Billable $ columns on the Employee and payroll Hours reports) also price a pinned client's hours at its pin, matching the generated invoice for everyone with a bill rate.
- **TIME BREAKDOWN ON THE INVOICE - OFF UNLESS YOU TURN IT ON, PER CLIENT.** By default an invoice says what the client is paying and nothing about the hours behind it: a monthly client sees the subscription line and any expense reimbursement line, with prices, nothing else. On the client page, **Time breakdown on the invoice** offers four levels of detail:
  - **One line per person (total hours)** - each person's total for the month.
  - **Per person, per day** - a line for each day someone worked.
  - **Per person, per week** - a line for each week someone worked.
  - **Every entry for the month** - one line per entry, with what was done.

  With any level on, **Show amounts on the breakdown** appears, adding what each line of time was worth. **The breakdown never changes what the client owes.** Every line it adds is $0.00 with no charge; it explains the invoice, it does not price it, so switching it on, off, or between levels is always safe on any invoice, and the total does not move. Hours show as a total - "4.00 hours" - never as clock-in and clock-out times. On an **Hourly** client, "one line per person" adds nothing - the invoice already bills one line per role title ("Bookkeeping Services", with the person's name added when two people share a role) with hours and money - while per day, per week and every entry still add detail; its charges are unaffected either way.
- This invoice's reimbursements: add out-of-pocket expenses (date, description, amount) - each becomes an invoice line. Recurring reimbursements supported.
- Reimbursed expenses can name the DATES THEY COVER and keep them current on their own. Set up on the client page (Recurring reimbursements): tick "The invoice wording names the dates this covers", write the wording ONCE with placeholders - `{range}`, `{start}`, `{end}`, `{description}` - and enter the first covered period by hand (e.g. July 13 to August 13). Every later invoice fills in its own cycle's dates: "QuickBooks Online - August 13 - September 13, 2026". The cycle turns on the day the first period ENDS; short months clamp (a 31st becomes the 28th in February) and the cycle returns to its own day the following month.
  - The window moves when an invoice is GENERATED, not on a calendar: voiding and regenerating a month reuses its window, so a rebuilt invoice covers the same dates as the first.
  - VOIDING un-bills the window: a month voided and never rebuilt goes back to unbilled, so the next invoice offers that same period.
  - Quarterly and annual expenses cover three and twelve months per cycle, matching their frequency.
  - Pause an expense (the pause button on its row) and it stops billing entirely, keeping its place - nothing advances while it sits out.
  - The invoice ASKS, rather than guessing, whenever the next window is not simply the one after the last (a skipped cycle, a paused expense switched back on, or a month generated behind one already billed): the line is flagged "Confirm the covered dates" in the month-run editor with a proposed period she can accept or edit. Until she answers, the invoice can be neither marked reviewed NOR sent - both are refused; voiding it is the way out if she does not want to answer.
  - What she confirms is what the next cycle counts forward from, including the day of the month: moving a covered period's end onto a different day moves the whole cycle onto that day rather than snapping back next month, unless a later month is already billed, which keeps the cycle where it was.
  - Confirming refreshes the invoice wording around the new dates - unless she typed her own wording on that line, which is left exactly as written. On a draft, reviewed or sent invoice (not void, paid or processing), "Change covered dates" on such a line sets From / To (Save dates) once her other unsaved edits are saved: the wording follows and next month continues from the end date. Only the latest month billed for that expense can change; on an earlier month the control reads "Dates are set by a later invoice - change them there." A first invoice uses the dates typed at setup, the usual reason to change them. On a reviewed invoice, a flagged line's Confirm dates stays available even with unsaved edits; it asks first, because confirming discards them.
  - An expense that does not use this bills exactly as it always did.
- Customize: adjust line items, the intro/footer notes and which client fields appear - on the PRINTED sheet only. These edits are session-only and never emailed: Email invoice always sends the stored invoice, so to change what a client receives, edit the lines in the month-run editor and save. Email invoice is unavailable while Customize is open.
- Email invoice: actually sends the STORED invoice (never the Customize version) for the selected client and month to that client's contacts on file, through the same rail as the month run's Send - not a mail draft. With no invoice yet it offers to build this client's (above); the invoice must be past Draft (mark it reviewed in the month run), otherwise the button explains what is missing instead of sending. Asks for confirmation first; reports who it went to and when. It sends the month's MONTHLY invoice, never a retainer invoice by accident: if the month's only live invoice is a retainer, it says no monthly invoice has been generated yet (send the retainer invoice from its row in the list) and sends nothing.
- Print invoice: first asks which month (defaulting to the month run's month) and says which sheet will print: that month's SAVED invoice, with its number and status, or a live preview from current time and rates when none exists. Customize open on the page's own month prints the on-screen sheet with the Customize edits instead. Then the browser's print dialog on a clean, print-formatted invoice sheet with firm branding - only the invoice prints, not the sidebar, billing queue or rest of the screen; print it or "Save as PDF". Printing emails nothing and saves nothing to the invoice.
  - Every Print button (the month-run editor's, History's, and this page's) produces this SAME sheet. The month run's and History's Print show the STORED invoice for that row; this page's shows that same stored invoice when the month has one, else the live per-client calculation. The emailed PDF is a separate server renderer sharing the line calculation and money formatter, so amounts and lines agree while presentation can differ slightly; it can carry what the sheet does not: a **PAID** banner once the invoice is marked paid, and a card payment's processing fee line. It is attached best-effort (above). To see the PDF and the email themselves, use **Preview** in the month run.
- Billing queue: all clients with their month total, ready to review.

Saving a month that already has a rate corrects it; saving an earlier month backfills a change you forgot to record.
  - **Which clients a saved rate reaches.** An hourly client bills each person's rate as it stood in the client's **rate month** (Client page -> Billing -> Hourly rates); saving a rate never moves a client's rate month. A rate reaches every hourly client whose rate month is its effective-from month or later (up to the next month this person already has a rate saved for). With the default (the current month) that is every client on the current month's rates (including any created or switched to Hourly this month, or already moved to this month or later); every other client stays put until moved at its review. To leave those clients alone, date the raise after their rate month.
  - **The exception: a person's FIRST rate.** Someone with no rate on file as of a client's rate month (hired after it, or given their first rate later) bills at the FIRST bill rate they were given, from the month it starts - including at clients whose rate month is earlier - never a later raise, held there until the client's rate month moves. For a month before their first rate starts, their hours bill like someone with no bill rate on file.

Owner-only to edit, informational - NEVER billed, never shown to staff. With no cost rate on file that person's time costs nothing: Cost cells read "-" and the assistant reports realization only. **Every saved cost rate carries an "effective from" date**, default today, and time is costed at the rate in force **on the day it was worked**, so a raise lands on its payday and never changes what a past recap says the work cost.
- How both rate boxes behave:
  - **Save is disabled while the box is empty** (a blank box never clears a rate). After a save, "Effective from" resets to the current month (bill) or today (cost).
  - The box shows the **newest version on file** - the latest effective-from, even a future month. Backfilling an older month does not change it.
  - **"Rate history"** lists every version with the month or date it starts from; a cost rate on file before dated rates existed reads "Since the start". **Only the newest version can be removed** (Remove sits on that row) - an older one is what past months were billed or costed at. To clear a rate entirely, remove versions one at a time, newest first; when the last one goes the rate reads blank.
  - **A bill-rate version cannot be removed while any client is pinned at or after its month** - including a month a client's rate history still bills a past period at. The page shows the reason as a sentence; move that client first.
  - **A person's only bill-rate version also cannot be removed while any hourly client is pinned before its month** (live, or in a month that client's rate history still prices) - that client bills the person's first rate from its start month until the client's review, so removing it would drop the client to its own hourly rate (often $0) on invoices not yet generated. To change a person's only rate, save its month again with the new amount instead.
- Roles: owner has everything; staff see their assigned clients, their own time, and ALL tasks for those clients (logging time against any of them), while editing/completing stays limited to tasks assigned to them. There is exactly one owner.

  - **"Why can't [person] log time?"** - it checks the two gates the timer itself applies and names what's stopping them: a **locked timesheet month** (only an owner can unlock it, on the Timesheet page), or an **earlier week** never submitted or sent back for changes (that person submits/resubmits it). It names every blocking week at once, says who fixes each, and says plainly when nothing is blocking them. It answers for logging time TODAY; backfilling an ended week is never gated, so only the month lock can stop that. Owners are exempt from both gates, and a removed team member is reported as such rather than as a gate.
  - **"Why did [client]'s checklist never show up?"** - it runs the same gate the generator uses and reports, per recurring checklist, whether it will generate next cycle and, if not, exactly which ingredient is missing: no client, no stages, no steps in the first stage, no months chosen (for "specific months"), a scheduled year that isn't this year, no next due date, or **switched off**. It also says whether the checklist has ever generated anything, and warns when one will generate but has nobody assigned or no Board column. With no name given, it lists every recurring checklist that will never generate - the silent kind.
  - **"What changed recently?"** - it reads the activity log for the last 7 days (or any window you ask for) and says who did what, in plain English. Narrow it to a client, a checklist, or a person to trace a surprise back to the change that caused it.
- Turning a **switched-off recurring checklist back on** is the one fix it can make from a diagnosis, as a confirmation card (switching it off again undoes it). For any OTHER missing ingredient (no steps, no due date, no client) it says exactly what to fix and where, without changing it.

## Updates (owner only)

- A tracker for pending feature updates and bug fixes - the owner's roadmap. Items come from requests this assistant drafts (chat -> "Send to Alex"; they land here) and from the owner adding them: title, type, **priority (set right in the add form; defaults to Medium)**, and a plain-language description.
- **"Just spitballing…" (Britt's Brain)**: for ideas that aren't requests yet. The button at the top opens a chat where the AI thinks it through WITH the owner - reflecting the idea back, asking a few questions per turn, never pushing toward a spec. When the idea has shape (or she clicks "Wrap it up & organize"), it offers an organized draft (The idea / What it could look like / Open questions / Why it matters); one click saves it to the **Britt's Brain** section - a parking spot for thinking, NOT the dev queue: left out of "Copy all" and the developer's queue runs until Alex moves one to Planned via the status dropdown. The full transcript is kept in the item's notes. If the AI is unavailable, "Save my notes as-is" still files the raw idea.
- **Just spitballing remembers.** The brainstorm is saved on the server as you go - close the window, come back tomorrow, or switch devices and it is where you left it. Past about thirty messages the earlier part is condensed into a running summary rather than dropped. Each finished brainstorm is remembered for the next - "like we talked about last time" works, because it sees the gist of past sessions plus the titles of everything parked in Britt's Brain. **"Start fresh"** (bottom of the window) tucks the current brainstorm away - asking first if there's anything in it - and starts a clean one; nothing is deleted, and the archived session is exactly what the AI recalls later.
- **When the AI is at capacity, spitballing says so instead of getting worse.** If the AI stumbles on a turn it first retries quietly without the older-sessions memory. If the provider is overloaded, the brainstorm shows "The AI is at capacity right now - give it a minute and try again. Your notes are safe." rather than answering with a weaker stand-in model. Wait a minute and re-send. (Other AI features - Refine for dev, the feedback read-back - do quietly use the stand-in, because their suggestions are reviewed before anything is saved.)
- Each item has a type (Feature / Bug / Improvement), a status (New -> Planned -> Planned (not near EOM) -> In Progress -> Needs answer -> Shipped -> Done, or Won't do), and a color-coded priority - Urgent (red), High (orange), Medium (blue), Low (slate).
- **Planned (not near EOM)**: a parking lane for planned items that touch a lot of the app. The developer's queue picks these up only MID-month (roughly the 6th-23rd), never in the month-end close window; move an item in or out with the status dropdown.
- **Needs your answer** (clarification loop): an item the developer can't build without a decision moves to "Needs answer" with the blocking question, and appears in an amber panel pinned ABOVE all sections with an answer box and "Answer & return to Planned", which stores the answer (shown as "Q: … - A: …" on its card) and puts it back in the Planned queue.
- Every Shipped item shows **when it went live** ("Shipped Jul 24 · 9:12 PM") next to its title; re-shipping (after a send-back) re-stamps the time.
- Layout: a TAB BAR across the top, one tab per status, each showing its COUNT. Shipped is the first tab and the default (just-shipped work awaiting her sign-off). Empty statuses keep their tab (showing 0); the "Hide Done / Won't do" toggle removes those two tabs entirely, and if the selected tab disappears the view falls back to the first one. Within a tab items are ordered by priority level (Urgent -> Low) and drag-to-rank within their level; dragging only re-ranks within the same status. Changing an item's status moves it to the matching tab; changing its priority moves it between levels.
- Ship + approve workflow: a pushed update is set to "Shipped" (violet badge; still open, awaiting sign-off; the Shipped tab reads "Awaiting your approval"). A Shipped item shows "Mark approved" (moves it to "Done", recording "Approved by <name> · <date>") AND "Not approved", which opens a reason box. "Send back" first runs an **AI read-back**: the assistant restates the reason in the owner's own terms ("So the change you want is …", or asks which of two readings she means); only after "Yes - send back" is it filed, returning to **Planned** with an amber "Not approved - <date>: <reason>" note plus the confirmed dev-ready rework spec. "Send back without the read-back" works when the AI is unavailable. An In-Progress item's whole card pulses.
- **"Walk me through it"** sits beside "Mark approved" and "Not approved" on every Shipped item. It opens a panel on the card with a plain-language walkthrough in four parts: **What changed** (a few sentences), **Where to find it** (the page and controls by name), **Try it** (a short numbered list to follow now), and **What did NOT change** (the scope of the approval). The AI writes it from the developer's shipped notes plus the matching part of this manifest - never from code or commit details - and it is SAVED with the item, so re-opening shows it instantly; "Regenerate" asks for a fresh one. Reading material only: it never approves, sends back, or changes status; if the AI is unavailable the panel says so and both review buttons still work.
- Editing an item: click the title or "Edit" to edit title + description in place; Save commits (no auto-save). Status, priority and type change at once from their dropdowns.
- "Refine for dev": the AI rewrites a rough item into an implementation-ready spec (Problem / Desired behavior / Where in the app / Acceptance); the owner accepts or discards it.
- "Copy for Claude Code" (per item) and "Copy all (prioritized)" put a paste-ready markdown spec on the clipboard for the developer's build tool.

## NOT supported (yet) - common asks

The app currently has NO:
- Client-facing portal (clients cannot log in or see anything - they can pay an invoice from its emailed link, but there is nothing to sign into)
- QuickBooks / Xero / bank-feed integration of any kind
- Automatic invoice sending on a schedule (sending is manual, per month)
- Document/file storage for client paperwork
- Payroll features
- Calendar sync (Google/Outlook)
- Native mobile app in an app store (but the web app is responsive AND installable - see "Install as an app": own window, own icon, phone home screen)
- Custom report builder (Reports/Productivity CSVs are the export surface)
- Multi-firm / multi-workspace support
- Public API or webhooks
- Email inbox integration

If the owner asks for one of these (or anything else missing), say it's not supported yet and offer to send Alex a feature request.
